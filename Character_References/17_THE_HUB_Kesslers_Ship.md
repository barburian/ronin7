# THE CAIRN — KESSLER'S SHIP (THE HUB)
*Worldbuilding & gameplay reference for the crew's persistent home base. Draws from `00_STORY_BIBLE.md` §4–5 and Ch01. Must not contradict canon; where it adds, additions are flagged.*

> **Name (canon):** *The Cairn.* A cairn is a pile of stones that both marks a grave and marks the way home — a salvager's heap that means *the dead are remembered* and *this is where you return.* It fits the tonal pillar (samurai elegy in a dead-tech galaxy; machines that remember). In dialogue, **Kessler just calls it "the rig"**; the crew comes to call it **the Hub.** *The Cairn* is its proper name — used in reference docs, on the hull, and the rare time anyone says it aloud.

---

## 1. ONE-LINE IDENTITY
A dead capital-class leviathan that a single salvager dragged home, gutted, and lives inside — a mountain of cold wreckage that the crew wakes back to life, deck by deck, as the family grows. Not a warship reborn. A graveyard that learns to breathe.

**Reconciliation with Ch01 (important):** in Chapter 1 the ship reads as a battered salvage barge a routine Dominion patrol boards without a second thought. That holds because **most of the hull is dark, unpowered, and scanning as scrap.** Kessler only ever lit the few compartments he needed: revival bay, medical bay, a jury-rigged command room, one hangar throat. The leviathan around those rooms is a cold, silent hulk. The patrol boards what looks like junk. The *grandeur is latent* — the game's job is to bring it online.

---

## 2. SCALE & SILHOUETTE
- **Class (original):** a derelict Dominion **fleet-tender / archive-hauler** — a logistics leviathan built to service warfleets and ferry the Program's living-archive caskets between depots. Kilometers, not meters. Think *a drifting iron canyon*, not a fighter.
- **Silhouette:** a long spine of stacked salvage — three or four original hull-sections welded nose-to-tail by Kessler, the seams armored in mismatched plate scavenged off a dozen dead ships. Ribbed, asymmetric, barnacled with sensor masts and grapple-cranes. It does not look designed. It looks *accreted*, like something that grew by swallowing other wrecks.
- **Read at a glance:** from outside it is unlovely and enormous — running dark, one or two lit ports like eyes in a skull. Lived-in, scavenged, patient. **Weight over spectacle.** When it finally powers up in Act IV, the silhouette that was a tomb becomes a fortress, and the reveal lands precisely *because* it spent the whole game looking dead.

---

## 3. HOW KESSLER CAME TO OWN IT *(addition — flagged)*
Kessler didn't buy it; he *salvaged* it and never left. He found the tender adrift in a wreck-field — power dead, crew long gone, its archive-holds full of sealed Program caskets it had been hauling when it died. He stripped it for parts, then realized the cheaper move was to live in the thing he was stripping. Over six years he welded other wrecks onto its spine, ran cabling by hand, and turned a corpse-ship into a one-man home. The casket that holds Ronin-7 in Ch1 is *exactly the kind of cargo this ship was built to carry* — a quiet, grim rhyme: the salvager's home was once a hearse for the very man it now shelters. *(Optional reveal beat for later chapters; ties the Hub directly into Ladder E — operatives-as-archives.)*

## 4. DECK-BY-DECK LAYOUT
*Ordered roughly bow→stern, lower→upper. Canon rooms (from Ch01) are marked **[CANON]**; the rest are additions consistent with the bible.*

### A. THE HANGAR THROAT *(primary hangar)*
The ship's open wound and its front door. A cavernous bay where Kessler's salvage-skiffs, the boarding shuttle, and later the crew's stolen/scavenged craft are docked. Grapple-cranes, fuel lines, a scarred deck stained with six years of work. Big enough to swallow a gunship. **Gameplay:** mission launch point — select a destination, kit out, deploy. As allies join, *their* ships dock here (Resh's smuggler runner, Vess's raider, etc.), and the bay fills up visibly with the size of the family.

### B. THE MAIN HOLD / SALVAGE BAY *(secondary hangar — **CANON: the Ch1 "main hold"**)*
The big lived-in workspace deeper in the hull where Kessler breaks down wrecks for parts — **this is the "main hold" the player tours in Ch1**: salvage everywhere, sorted and half-stripped (copper, stripped hull plate, graded piles), a grimy viewport onto the wreck-field, the dented cups and worn workbench of a man alone. Cutting rigs, scrap-sorting bins, half-dismembered drones. **Gameplay:** the resource/crafting economy lives here — salvage hauled back from missions becomes Hub upgrades and weapon mods. Morrigan (Ch6) eventually co-opts a corner of it for her engineering work.

### C. REVIVAL BAY **[CANON]**
Where the game opens. The table Kessler kept Ronin-7's body on for three weeks; failed and salvaged med-tech wired into one working unit. Quiet, clinical, haunted. **Gameplay:** narrative anchor; the "you came back from the dead here" room. Becomes a respawn/checkpoint shrine thematically.

### D. MEDICAL BAY **[CANON]**
Adjacent to revival; where the Ch1 boarding fight happens in cramped quarters. Patch-up station for the crew. **Gameplay:** heal/loadout node; later staffed by Iris and (Ch13) Dr. Heris, whose presence upgrades it into a real surgery.

### E. THE COMMAND ROOM → THE BRIDGE **[CANON, expanded]**
In Ch1 it's a single battered console and a half-dead **ship-drone** that announces the handler's hologram. **Addition:** this is one lit corner of a far larger **dead bridge** — tiers of cold control stations the crew reignites over the game. By Act III it's the war-room: a central holo-table where missions are planned, the Concord Engine's map is assembled, and the roster argues. **Gameplay:** mission select / story hub / the "talk to your crew before the next op" space. The battered ship-drone is the persistent Hub AI/quest-giver mascot.

### F. THE ARCHIVE-HOLDS *(the ship's dark heart — addition, flagged)*
Deep in the hull: the original fleet-tender's cargo — rows of sealed living-archive caskets it was hauling when it died, still down there, still holding the kept shadow-AIs of the dead. Kessler walled them off and doesn't go in. **Thematic payload:** the Hub is *literally a living archive* like the katana and the operatives — the kept are *here.* This dovetails with **Ladder E** (sword-shadow → operatives-carry-shadows → living archives → Engine lattice → shadows freed Ch16). When the Concord Engine dies in Ch16 and the shadow-AIs are freed, *these caskets are part of what's freed.* The crew has been living on top of a grave the whole time. *(Strong optional through-line — recommend wiring Sable (Ch9, the archivist) to this room.)*

### G. CREW QUARTERS / THE COMMONS
A long gut of the ship Kessler never needed and the crew slowly claims — bunks, a galley, a scarred mess table. **This is where "every ally stays aboard the Hub" physically lives** (bible §4). Starts as one man's empty corridors; fills with the Ten and the companions until it's loud and crowded and *home.* **Gameplay:** ally hub — talk to recruited crew between missions, trigger bond/loyalty scenes, watch the space populate as a visible measure of progress. Mira's stowaway corner; Iris's workbench; Resh's smuggling cache.

### H. THE IRON DOJO *(Morrigan's, Ch6 — lives aboard after recruitment)*
Per bible §4 every ally stays aboard, so after Ch6 the "Iron Dojo" is a space *within* the Hub — a stripped cargo bay Morrigan turns into a training/engineering hall and names, in memory and defiance, after the Dominion training citadel she helped Ronin-7 empty and bring down. **Gameplay:** combat-upgrade and skill-practice node; the katana's shadow-AI work and the crew's training happen here.

### I. ENGINEERING / THE SPINE
The welded seams between the original hull-sections — exposed cabling, reactor access, the literal joints where Kessler fused wreck to wreck. Dangerous, half-pressurized, atmospheric traversal space. **Gameplay:** the "ship is alive and barely holding together" texture; optional explorable; powers the deck-by-deck reawakening system (§5).

## 5. THE HUB WAKES UP — progression system *(core design hook)*
The single best thing this ship can do mechanically: **start dead and come alive as the story does.** At Ch1 only ~four compartments have power. Each recruited ally and each story beat **lights another section of the leviathan.** The Hub the player ends with in Ch16 is unrecognizable from the cold tomb they woke up in.

| Trigger | What wakes |
|---|---|
| Ch1 start | Revival bay, medical bay, main hold/salvage bay, one command console, the airlock dock (bare minimum — the only rooms Ch1 tours) |
| Ch2 — Resh, Iris, Mira aboard | Crew commons begins to fill; Resh's runner docks |
| Ch6 — Morrigan | Engineering comes online; Morrigan's training bay (the Iron Dojo) opens; real upgrade bench |
| Ch7 — Coral Vex / Wraith line | Archive-holds acknowledged; the ship's "memory" subplot opens |
| Ch9–10 — Gryph, Sable, Cassie-04, Vess | Lower hangar fully operational; war-room holo-table assembled |
| Ch13 — Dr. Heris | Medical bay → full surgery; reactor restored to full power |
| Ch16 | The whole leviathan lit — the tomb is a fortress; shadow-AIs freed |

**Design payoff:** the Hub is a *visible save file.* Players read their own progress in how much of the ship is breathing. Mirrors the protagonist's arc — a dead thing brought lucid, then whole.

## 6. TECH & AESTHETIC
- **Look:** dead-tech, not chrome. Salvaged plating, hand-run cable, cold-fusion lamps, holograms that flicker because the projectors were scavenged off three different ships. Where it's powered, the light is warm and low; where it isn't, it's black and frosted. **Lived-in, not showroom.**
- **Sound:** the deep tidal groan of an oversized hull flexing; distant unpowered decks ticking as they cool; the ship-drone's chirp. Quiet menace.
- **The "techie" promise, kept honestly:** the *capability* is high-end (capital sensors, archive-grade data systems, hangar capacity, a real reactor) but the *presentation* is jury-rigged. It's powerful because it was once a fleet-tender — not because Kessler is rich. Cool through scale and history, not polish.
- **Smell/texture beats (for environment art):** ozone and coolant, old blood in the revival bay, machine oil in the hangars, the cold-metal silence of the dead decks.

---

## 7. GAMEPLAY HUB FUNCTIONS *(consolidated)*
- **Mission select / deploy** — Hangar throat + war-room holo-table.
- **Upgrade bench / weapon & katana mods** — Salvage bay + Iron Dojo (post-Ch6).
- **Heal / loadout** — Medical bay.
- **Save / checkpoint** — Revival bay (thematic respawn shrine).
- **Crafting economy** — Salvage hauled to the lower hangar.
- **Ally bonds / loyalty scenes** — Crew commons.
- **Story progression read-out** — how much of the ship is lit (§5).
- **Wanted-level / heat** — the Hub's running-dark stealth is *why* it isn't found; restoring power is a risk/reward tension the heat system can use.

---

## 8. TONE GUARDRAILS
- Keep it **unlovely and enormous**, never sleek. Spectacle is earned in Act IV, not given in Act I.
- The ship **remembers** — the archive-holds, the revival table, the welded scars of other dead ships. Every cool feature should also be a little haunted. *Earned tenderness; bodies and machines that remember.*
- It is **Kessler's** before it is the crew's. Its heart is a father waiting to bring his daughter home. The grandeur serves that, or it's just a set.
