SETTING / ENVIRONMENT — WHERE THIS CHAPTER TAKES PLACE
Reference for art, lighting, level, and audio direction. Chapter 1 was one dead
ship in empty black; Chapter 2 is the opposite — Velorum, an auction-world that
never stops moving, where the only quiet is the quiet you buy. Treat the crowd as
the fifth character: a sea of buyers and sold, indifferent, churning, loud enough
to drown a scream and used to drowning them. Where the rig was forgotten, Velorum
is the place that does the forgetting — for a fee.

VELORUM (the auction-world)
- A market built down into itself: tier on tier of stalls, gantries, and cages
  bored into an old mining moon, lit by signage and slaughter-bright auction lamps.
  Heat, noise, the smell (implied) of bodies and ozone and frying oil. Color
  everywhere, none of it clean. Up top it sells weapons and wonders; the deeper
  you go, the more it sells people and pretends it doesn't.
- The Dominion does not police Velorum. It feeds it. Operatives, salvage, the
  dead and their paperwork all flow through here. The place runs on the fiction
  that everything has a price and nothing has a name.
- The market has a voice. A syndicate overseer's box looks down on the Auction
  floor; when the floor's order is disturbed, that voice comes over the house PA —
  never raised, never hurried. It is Velorum's version of the Handler: the calm
  thing that owns the loud room.

  BEAT 0 — ABOARD THE RIG (the pre-Velorum briefing)
  - The one location that isn't Velorum: Kessler's salvage rig from Chapter 1,
    the wreck-field still turning slow in the ports, the hull mostly dark. A single
    lit command room, one cracked nav-console. The crew is two — Kessler and the
    man he pulled out of a coffin — and the partnership is hours old. This is the
    chapter's only quiet, and the only scene before the noise: where the lead to
    Velorum is set and the course is laid in.

  BEAT 1 — THE UNDERMARKET (descent + the lead)
  - The crew's entry tier: choke-narrow lanes, hanging signage, hawkers, vendor
    stalls jammed wall to wall. A river of people. No sightlines, no exits a man
    can trust. Built to make a newcomer small.
  - A broker's stall near the bottom of the tier: barred, screened, a paranoid man
    behind glass who deals in operative records and trusts no one to his face.
  - This is where the trail forks: the broker is a wall; the only door through him
    is a name, Resh.

  BEAT 2 — THE AUCTION FLOOR (sparing Resh) — ALLY #1
  - The great Auction: a tiered amphitheater pit, a raised block under hard light,
    a caller's pulpit, a packed gallery of bidders. The single biggest open space
    in the chapter, and the most watched. Everything that happens here happens in
    front of a thousand eyes — and one overseer's box above them all.
  - Built for the branch: cover and chokes for a brawl OR a bidder's rail and a
    paddle for the economy path. Resh is worked into the floor as a fixer/runner.
  - The Mira plant: a small figure in the gallery, watching Resh, not the block.

  BEAT 3 — THE HOLDING CELLS (Iris freed)
  - The cells: a flooded-dark sub-tier reached by one of Resh's back-channels — a
    maintenance crawl the market forgot it had. Collateral cages, collar-tags,
    syndicate enforcers walking rounds. Wet, low, ugly. The place the noise above
    is designed to cover. Iris is caged here; the reunion with Kessler opens on
    the comm wire, his voice in everyone's ear from the barge above.

  BEAT 4 — THE RECORDS-VAULT (the failed execution) — THE REVEAL
  - The broker's records-vault: a cold archive of the dead. Disposal manifests of
    executed operatives, jettisoned with their bodies and bought here as salvage —
    the same trade that delivered Ronin-7's casket to Kessler. Racked drives, a
    single disposal terminal, a diagnostic table built to read an operative's
    termination record: what fired, what closed, what the body logged on its way out.
  - Intimate, sealed, final. After a chapter of crowds, the two people who matter
    and one machine telling the truth.

  BEAT 5 — BACK ABOARD KESSLER'S SHIP (the Hub / homecoming + Mira found)
  - The ship's hold, Velorum already dropping away behind them: crates, tie-downs,
    a fold of tarp by the storage lockers big enough for a small body to hide
    behind. After the cells and the vault, the first room in the chapter that is
    theirs, and the chapter's one breath of quiet.
  - Everyone is aboard and face to face for the first time — no comm between them.
    Kessler finally holds the daughter he only had on a wire below. Resh, counting
    heads out of old habit, crosses to the storage and finds the stowaway: Mira,
    the child from the gallery, who followed him home. He brings her out to all of
    them, and she is kept — every recruited ally now stays aboard this ship, the
    persistent home base.
  - Closes on the settling quiet, the wrapped katana laid on the rack — the exact
    room and object Chapter 3 opens on, where the blade wakes only for Ronin-7.

INTRUDING / RECURRING ELEMENTS
- The Dominion (felt, not seen): its disposal trade is why the dead and their
  paperwork wash up on Velorum at all. The market is its midden.
- The syndicates: Velorum's actual muscle — enforcers, brokers, the bodyguard,
  the overseer in the glass box. Not soldiers. Owners of stock, protecting
  inventory, collecting later.
- Kessler and the barge: he stays with the ship through the Velorum legs (Beats
  1–4), his voice riding the crew's channel; the Iris reunion opens on that wire
  in the cells. It completes face to face in Beat 5, once the crew is back aboard.

TONE OF PLACE
Noise, commerce, and a machine that eats people and files the receipts. Velorum is
loud the way the rig was silent, and for the same reason: both are built to make
a man disappear. The rig hid one body by accident. Velorum hides thousands on
purpose, and charges admission. Earn the tenderness against that.

BEAT 4 — THE FAILED EXECUTION (Dialogue) — THE REVEAL

[Interior: the broker's records-vault. Cold, sealed, racked with drives — an archive of the dead. Disposal manifests of executed operatives, jettisoned with the bodies and bought here as salvage. One disposal terminal. A diagnostic table built to read an operative's termination record — what fired, what closed, what the body logged on its way out. The auction roar is gone entirely. Resh works the outer door; Iris goes straight to the terminal; Ronin-7 stands among the racks of the cataloged dead. The broker's bodyguard is between them and the terminal.]

Speaker: Resh
Line: Door's mine. The thing standing in front of the terminal is the broker's. He buys muscle the way he buys paper. Don't expect it to talk.
seconds: 9
voice: Resh, low, working the lock-panel, a glance at the bodyguard.

[The bodyguard says nothing. A wall of a man steps off the terminal and into the floor, blocking it. No threats. Just inventory, defending inventory.]

[PLAYABLE COMBAT — MINI-BOSS: THE BROKER'S BODYGUARD. A single heavy, silent opponent fought in the close confines of the vault, around the racks of the dead. Slower and far more punishing than the cell enforcers; a real wall. No dialogue during — only the fight, the racks, the cold. Iris and Resh take cover; this is Ronin-7's blade against the last thing between him and his own file. Tone target: hard-won, no spectacle, the dead watching.]

[CUTSCENE CAP — on clear: the bodyguard down. The vault silent. Ronin-7 stands breathing over him for a beat, then turns to the racks. Iris is already at the terminal, hands moving.]

Speaker: Iris
Line: These aren't records. They're people. Dead operatives, jettisoned with their own disposal paper, then bought back here as salvage.
seconds: 8
voice: Iris, at the terminal, the tech reading the system aloud, faintly sick at what it is. Fast, precise.

Speaker: Ronin-7
Line: The same trade that put me in front of Kessler. A box thrown out as garbage.
seconds: 6
voice: Ronin-7, quiet, looking at the racks of the dead. The connection landing flat and cold.

Speaker: Resh
Line: I've spent my whole life smuggling the living out through a building that runs on the dead.
seconds: 7
voice: Resh, grim, by the door. A man seeing the floor under his whole life named at last.

[Iris's hands stop. Something has surfaced on the terminal. Her face changes.]

Speaker: Iris
Line: ...There's one here under your designation. Filed with the dead. It's yours.
seconds: 6
voice: Iris, careful, low. She knows what room she's about to walk them into.

[Ronin-7 crosses to the terminal. He looks at the file the way he looks at everything — like terrain, like a wound he's measuring for depth, not for pain.]

Speaker: Ronin-7
Line: Read it.
seconds: 1
voice: Ronin-7, flat. A man asking the dead what they wrote down about him.

[Iris scrolls the med-log. The systems-mind reads ahead of her mouth, and what it finds stops her cold for a beat before she says it.]

Speaker: Iris
Line: This is your termination order. Executed. Signed off.
seconds: 5
voice: Iris, reading, then a held beat. The clinical words landing wrong in her own ears.

Speaker: Iris
Line: The killswitch discharged. Full sequence.
seconds: 3
voice: Iris, quieter, the weight of it arriving. A beat before and after.

Speaker: Ronin-7
Line: Then why am I reading it?
seconds: 2.5
voice: Ronin-7, level. The simplest question in the room, and the one that breaks it open.

[Iris looks at the diagnostic, then runs it again, because the first read can't be right. It is.]

Speaker: Iris
Line: Because it didn't take. It fired into your skull, full discharge, and you just... kept breathing.
seconds: 8
voice: Iris, the tech overtaken by the human, staring at the screen. Disbelief and something like awe.

[A silence. Resh has stopped working the door; even he understands he's hearing a thing that doesn't happen. Ronin-7's hand rises slowly to his throat, finds the old scar there — the one Kessler noticed on the table three weeks ago, closed clean and opened again — and stops. For the first time, the arithmetic doesn't come.]

[Iris looks up from the terminal, straight at him, the meaning of it widening past the one body in front of her.]

Speaker: Iris
Line: Do you understand what that means? Every one of you has this. A leash in the skull. Yours is the only one on record that failed.
seconds: 10
voice: Iris, low and certain, the systems-mind delivering the size of it. Ladder A, rung 1 — the first true crack in the cage.

Speaker: Ronin-7
Line: No.
seconds: 1
voice: Ronin-7, quiet. Correcting her, gently, the colder thought already moving behind it.

Speaker: Ronin-7
Line: Mine's the only one they've found that failed.
seconds: 4
voice: Ronin-7, low, certain. The relief curdling into the larger thing in the same breath: there are others, still leashed, and the cage is not as absolute as they were all built to believe.

[The words sit in the cold vault. Iris and Resh look at him, and at each other, the implication spreading between them.]

Speaker: Iris
Line: ...You think there are more of you. Ones that slipped and nobody caught it.
seconds: 5
voice: Iris, the tech already chasing the consequence, voice hushed.

Speaker: Ronin-7
Line: I think a leash that broke once on me can break on others. I don't know why mine slipped. But the men who built it don't know it can slip at all. That's the one thing they can't be allowed to keep believing.
seconds: 13
voice: Ronin-7, working the logic out loud, the same flat arithmetic he uses on everything — but aimed, for the first time, at a future instead of a kill. He knows what happened, not yet why.

[On the comm, Kessler's voice, quiet, having heard it all from above.]

Speaker: Kessler (Comm)
Line: I pulled a man out of a coffin who'd already been executed. I knew the box didn't hold. I didn't know nothing was ever supposed to come out of it at all.
seconds: 11
voice: Kessler, over comm, low and shaken. The mystery of the table three weeks ago given its true size.

[Ronin-7 takes the drive from the terminal. He looks once more at the racks of the cataloged dead, every one of them a file like his that did take.]

Speaker: Ronin-7
Line: Copy it. The file, and the others. Every leash in this room.
seconds: 5
voice: Ronin-7, to Iris, quiet and certain. The first order of a man who has stopped running and started hunting.

Speaker: Iris
Line: Copying. It'll take both of us a lifetime to read what's in here.
seconds: 4
voice: Iris, hands moving, the tech with a purpose now, the conscience of the crew finding its work.

Speaker: Resh
Line: Then it's a good thing you found a crew. Door's clear. Same routes out as in. Let's get the kid and Kessler's girl home before this market notices what it just sold us for free.
seconds: 12
voice: Resh, the smuggler back at the helm, but lit now from under by the idealist. Warm, dry, ready.

[Ronin-7 sheaths the blade. He pauses at the lip of the route up, the drive in his hand, and looks back one last time at the racked dead — every file a leash that held.]

Speaker: Ronin-7
Line: All these held. Somewhere the ones that didn't are still walking. Still leashed. Not knowing they could break.
seconds: 8
voice: Ronin-7, quiet, the relief in his hand and the dread past it in the same breath. The question the chapter leaves open, carried on his own face.

[He turns for the route up — toward the ship, the daughter returned, the child kept, the leash that failed copied in his hand, and the others still out there in the dark.]

Speaker: Ronin-7
Line: Let's go back to the ship.
seconds: 1.5
voice: Ronin-7, quiet — an echo of the rig, but the room behind him is full now, not empty, and the thing ahead of him is no longer a destination but a hunt.

[The crew moves up the smuggler's channel. Above them Velorum roars on, selling the dead and the living, unaware that one of its files just walked out breathing. SMASH TO BLACK.]

[Interior: Kessler's ship, the hold. The auction and the vault are behind them; Velorum is already falling away in the ports. The crew comes up the ramp into the ship's quiet — crates, tie-downs, the same fold of tarp in the corner. For the first time this chapter the noise is gone and the room is theirs. Iris stops in the middle of the open floor, unused to space with no bars in it.]

Speaker: Iris
Line: It's so quiet up here. I forgot quiet.
seconds: 3
voice: Iris, half to herself, the cage still on her skin. The chapter's one breath before the next turn.

[Kessler is there. Not a voice on a wire now — the man, crossing the hold to her. Six years stop in the space between them. He takes her face in both hands like he's checking she's real.]

Speaker: Kessler
Line: Six years I had a voice on a wire and a coffin where you should have been. Stand still. Let me look at you with my own eyes.
seconds: 11
voice: Kessler, low and broken open, face to face at last. The relief of the comm made solid, almost too big for the room.

Speaker: Iris
Line: I told you I'd come back. I earned the trip.
seconds: 4
voice: Iris, a thread of her father's dryness holding the tears off. The bond, now standing in the same room.

[Resh hangs back from the reunion, giving it air. But the smuggler in him is counting, the way he always counts, and the number comes out wrong. He looks toward the storage at the back of the hold.]

Speaker: Resh
Line: Hold the moment. I came up that ramp with one more set of footsteps than I should have. Stay put.
seconds: 7
voice: Resh, low, the runner's instinct pricking. Already moving toward the back of the hold.

[Resh crosses to the storage lockers, the fold of tarp. He stands over it a beat, then pulls it back. Behind it, small and still, the child from the gallery. Mira.]

Speaker: Resh
Line: ...No. No, you do not get to be down here. How did you get past me.
seconds: 6
voice: Resh, the smuggler's calm cracking into something protective and alarmed. He knows exactly what she is.

[The child says nothing at first. She looks at Resh, not at the strangers behind him. She knows him.]

Speaker: Mira
Line: You're Resh. You're the one who gets us out.
seconds: 4
voice: Female, a child, maybe nine. Quiet, watchful, certain. Few words, each one chosen. Not frightened, decided.

Speaker: Resh
Line: You know my face.
seconds: 2
voice: Resh, gone quiet, crouching to her level. The legend hearing it spoken back to him.

Speaker: Mira
Line: I saw you on the floor. You weren't running. You were with them. So I followed.
seconds: 6
voice: Mira, simple and sure. A child's logic that cuts straight through.

[Resh stands, and walks her out of the storage into the open hold, into the light, in front of all of them. Kessler. Iris. Ronin-7. The child stops at the edge of the group, taking the room in. For a second she's just a small kid in a strange place, and the certainty flickers.]

Speaker: Mira
Line: ...Is the loud part over? Up there. It's loud.
seconds: 3
voice: Mira, smaller now, the watchfulness slipping to show the child under it. Real fear, in a child's plain words.

[Kessler turns from his daughter to the child Resh is leading in. A father twice over in one day, the new softness in him surfacing.]

Speaker: Kessler
Line: ...That's a child. Resh, that is a child standing in my hold.
seconds: 4
voice: Kessler, quiet, wary, the protectiveness already ahead of the worry.

Speaker: Resh
Line: She stowed away. Off the auction floor. She's market stock that walked off the shelf on her own legs.
seconds: 7
voice: Resh, low, looking at the child like she's the whole argument for everything he does.

Speaker: Kessler
Line: I pulled one of mine out of a cage today. I am not in the business of turning a child back toward one. Not on this ship.
seconds: 9
voice: Kessler, the father stepping up, no comm between him and the kid now. Steadier for having something small to protect.

[Mira doesn't argue. She moves a step closer to Resh, and then, watching, a step toward Ronin-7. Reading him the way he reads everything. Ronin-7 crouches. For a moment the killer and the child take each other's measure.]

Speaker: Mira
Line: Are you going to send me back.
seconds: 3
voice: Mira, flat, watchful. Not pleading. Bracing, the same brace Resh wore on the floor, in a smaller body.

Speaker: Ronin-7
Line: No.
seconds: 1
voice: Ronin-7, immediate, certain. No arithmetic in it. The first thing he hasn't had to calculate.

[A beat. He doesn't soften his voice. He doesn't have to.]

Speaker: Ronin-7
Line: Nobody puts you back. You stay with the ship. You stay where Kessler can see you. But nobody puts you back.
seconds: 9
voice: Ronin-7, level, final. A line drawn somewhere deep and not negotiable.

Speaker: Resh
Line: ...That's twice today you've done a thing your kind doesn't do.
seconds: 4
voice: Resh, quiet, looking at the operative with something close to wonder.

Speaker: Kessler
Line: Then she stays. She stays aboard, with me, where I can see her, same as Iris. I have the room and I have the reason.
seconds: 9
voice: Kessler, the resistance gone, the guardian settling into it. A man who has decided what this ship is for.

[Mira looks once more at Resh, accepting it, and lets Iris draw her gently toward the bunks at the front of the hold. The crew is whole and aboard, and the only thing left to do is breathe. Resh watches them go and shakes his head at the shape of it.]

Speaker: Resh
Line: A salvage rat, a freed tech, a stowaway, and me. The market's whole lost-and-found, riding home on one ship.
seconds: 8
voice: Resh, wry, but the idealist's warmth right under it.

[Ronin-7 stands a moment in the settling quiet, the drive from the vault still in his pocket, the room behind him full where the rig was empty. He slings the wrapped katana off his shoulder and lays it on the rack by the bench. The ship runs on, Velorum dropping away in the dark.]

Speaker: Ronin-7
Line: Get some rest. Velorum can keep the rest of it.
seconds: 3
voice: Ronin-7, low, an echo of the rig, but the room behind him is full now, not empty. A homecoming he wasn't built to feel.

[He sets his hand off the grip and steps away from the rack. The hold goes quiet around the four of them and the child, and for once the quiet is theirs. The wrapped katana rests on the rack, waiting. SMASH TO BLACK.]
