SETTING / ENVIRONMENT — WHERE THIS CHAPTER TAKES PLACE
Reference for art, lighting, level, and audio direction. Chapter 5 took him to one
of his old killings and made him stand inside it. Chapter 6 takes him to the place
that made the hand that did the killing. The whole chapter unfolds at the IRON
DOJO, a Dominion training citadel carved across three granite peaks of a mountain
spur, on a world with a climate like old Earth: breathable air, a real sun, weather
that turns, pine forest cloaking the lower slopes, snow on the high ridges. The
Dominion built it beautiful on purpose. Terraced training yards step down the
mountainsides; aqueducts thread the rock; bells ring the hours over a central
muster ground, the Iron Yard. The children raised here are meant to believe this is
a home and a school, not the front end of a machine that erases them. It is the
most monstrous thing in the saga precisely because it looks like the kindest.
(Full ship layout: 17_THE_HUB_Kesslers_Ship.md.)

Each peak is crowned by a tower, and each tower is a stage of the making. High
bridges and a cable-tram span the gorges between them, over the central works where
the citadel's reactors, archives, and the keeper-engineer's quarters are sunk into
the mountain's spine. Treat the place as a wound dressed up as a cradle: the warmth
of the architecture is the horror, not a relief from it. The light is golden, the
air is clean, the bells are gentle, and underneath all of it the machine is eating
names. Nothing here should look evil. That is the point.

The chapter moves through five guided segments with a free-order core, climbing
from a forest slope up into the engineering spine and out across three peaks.

  SEGMENT 0 — THE CAIRN (the briefing, before the drop)
  - Not the mountain: Kessler's ship, the command room, more of the dead bridge lit
    than ever. The salvaged holo-table throws up a mountain citadel turning slowly
    in pale projection, three peaks and a central yard. This time the target is not
    a lead the crew dug up; it is one Resh has carried for years and finally laid on
    the table. The smuggler is not reading a job. He is settling a debt. Where the
    informant is named, Mera prices the garrison, Iris wants the tech, Kessler keeps
    Mira sealed aboard, and Echo, for Cipher alone, names the place for what it is.

  SEGMENT 1 — THE DROP & THE CLIMB (forest slope → mountain face) — TRAVERSAL
  - The boarding shuttle cannot near the towers; the citadel's air-defenses own the
    sky. It drops Cipher low and far, on the forested lower slope under a real,
    weathering sky, and pulls off. There is no gate to walk. The only route to
    Morrigan the garrison does not watch is the mountain itself. A movement-and-
    tension beat going up: wall-runs, ledge-jumps between aqueduct spans, climbs
    along the curtain wall, drops past patrol-lit terraces, weather and loose rock as
    hazard. Echo reads the rock and calls the routes, the mirror of the Drovis
    descent, only now it is open air and a long fall instead of black water.

  SEGMENT 2 — MORRIGAN'S ROOM (the engineering spine) — THE KILL-LIST
  - The keeper-engineer's hidden quarters sunk into the central works, lit by the
    cold glow of cracked slates and Program tech she has been reverse-engineering for
    years. Morrigan does not startle when he comes through the window; she has been
    waiting a long time for someone like him. Where she lays out the citadel the way
    only its engineer can, hands him the kill-list, and the three towers unlock for
    the player to take in any order.

  SEGMENT 3 — THE THREE TOWERS (player chooses the order)
  - Three self-contained peaks, each a stage of the making, each defended by its own
    cadre and crowned by its master. Parkour across the curtain walls and bridges to
    reach each tower's mouth, fight through the adult garrison, kill the master. The
    Cradle (Intake) and Matron Hespa; the Proving (Trial) and Drillmaster Caradoc;
    the Vesting (Graduation) and Master Kaelen. The children are the rescue, never
    the target. The blade is for the cadre, the garrison, and the machines. An
    optional mercy beat exists only for the Vesting's vested cadets, the ones most
    like him.

  SEGMENT 4 — KAELEN'S CONFESSION (the Vesting, top of the tower) — REVEAL
  - The last master to fall, dying, says the thing the whole Program is built to
    keep buried: the hollowness was not a failure, it was cut in on purpose, and the
    seam never fully holds. Where Ronin-7 is told, by the man who built men like him,
    that his mercy is not damage done to him but the flaw they designed in and prayed
    would never open. The saga's one disclosure of this truth. It must land.

  SEGMENT 5 — THE EVACUATION / THE IRON YARD (exfil) — ALLY #3 + SEEDS
  - All three masters dead, Cipher returns to Morrigan. With the towers leaderless
    she triggers a full evacuation and rigs the citadel to come down behind them. The
    eldest trainees lead the younger ones out down the forest roads toward a normal
    life none of them were meant to have; Resh's name opens the doors at the bottom.
    Morrigan walks out with Cipher, not with the children, and becomes Ally #3.
    Where she names the two unease that shape everything after: a hand outside the
    Program steering it, and one insider at the root of the work she means to find.

INTRUDING / RECURRING ELEMENTS
- THE CITADEL ITSELF (the kindness): golden light, gentle bells, clean air, terraced
  gardens, children in ordered lines. The chapter's atmosphere and its accusation
  both. Treat the beauty as the threat. Every warm detail is a leash dressed as a
  home. The art should make the player uneasy that it is this lovely.
- THE BELLS: rung over the Iron Yard to order the hours and the children. They are a
  recurring motif: gentle and regular through the climb and the towers, then in the
  evacuation they ring wrong, a single long unbroken call, the school's own alarm
  turned into the sound of it being emptied.
- THE CHILDREN (the rescue, never the target): trainees of every age, from the
  youngest in the Cradle to the near-finished cadets of the Vesting. Non-combatant
  objectives throughout. The blade never rises to them. The mercy of the whole
  chapter is redirected into them; they are what is saved when the masters die.
- ECHO (the katana's shadow-AI): heard by Ronin-7 alone, wired to his optic feed,
  sees what he sees. Calls him Cipher. The wry warmth of Ch4 is allowed back at the
  edges of the climb, then drops to bare witness in the towers and the confession.
  This is the building he was poured in, and Echo rode out the front of it the same
  as he did. The one voice that knows what this place is from the inside.

TONE OF PLACE
Chapter 5 asked what it cost the people the weapon did not spare. Chapter 6 asks
where the weapon was made, and answers that the place is a school with gardens and
bells. The horror is not a dungeon; it is a kindness running a machine. The arc of
the chapter climbs out of the cost and into the source: from a forest slope under a
real sun, up a beautiful mountain, into halls where the youngest are stripped of
their names so gently they thank the hands that do it. There is no monster at the
top with a monster's face. There is a soft woman certain she is merciful, a hard man
certain he is necessary, and an architect who, dying, finally says the truth out
loud: they did not break him by accident, and the one human thing left in him is the
thing they feared most. The chapter ends with the kindest-looking evil in the saga
coming down in fire while the children it was eating walk free into the trees.
