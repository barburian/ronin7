# RONIN-7 — STORY TIMELINE

*The running tracker for the VR game's story. Scope: **the complete story** — Chapters Ch01–Ch16, all four Acts: Act I, Act II, Act III (the gathering of the scattered archives and the clone-reveal climax: Rustfang deep → Ninefold ledger → bone-canyon origin → cryo-vault fracture), and Act IV (the maker, Dr. Heris, the completion of the ten, and the finale — the Iron Sepulcher, reclaiming Soren, and the Throne of Ashes). Per-chapter blocks count chapters with dialogue scripts; cut Ch14 and merged Ch15 are omitted by design.*

**How to use this file.** Two views of the same story: a **chronological timeline** (everything in story-time order, backstory first), then a **per-chapter breakdown** (one block per chapter), then **quick-reference tables**. This file is a tracker, not canon law — `00_STORY_BIBLE.md` is the source of truth; nothing here may contradict it.

**Maintenance.** The story is complete through the finale. Keep the reveal-ladder tracker in sync with Bible §10 (escalate one rung per chapter; never disclose a fact twice).

> **Naming note.** Narration/serial = **Ronin-7**. Codename **Cipher** is spoken aloud from Ch04 on (Echo uses "Cipher," never "7"). The katana's shadow-AI is **Echo** (called "Shadow" until he names it in Ch03). Home base is **The Cairn** (the Hub). "Kael Vor" is a planted cover name, exposed in Ch05. His true **birth name, Soren**, is recovered in the finale (Ch16).

## 1. CHRONOLOGICAL TIMELINE (story-time order)

### Backstory anchors (before the game opens)
- **~6 years ago** — Ronin-7, on a Program operation, **spares Kessler** instead of killing him. The mercy debt that later saves his own life.
- **~3 months ago — Kethel-7.** On the orphanage-erasure mission on the planet Kethel-7, Ronin-7's conditioned conscience breaks. He refuses the order, too late to save the caretaker and children. He is the **first operative to defect.** His handler **Khall** agrees he is right ("I know") and triggers his **killswitch** anyway. The discharge **fires and fails.** *(Witnessed by the shadow-AI riding his eyes; relived as playback in Ch04, not shown live.)*
- **~3 weeks ago** — The Dominion seals his body in a casket and jettisons it. **Kessler salvages** the drifting casket (no wreck nearby) and begins a three-week revival, repaying the six-year debt.

### Present day (the game's events, in order)

**Ch01 — aboard The Cairn**
1. Ronin-7 **wakes lucid** on the revival table: no name, fractured memory, lethal reflexes. Has Kessler by the throat before Kessler talks him down.
2. Kessler explains the salvage and the debt.
3. A **Dominion patrol boards**; a visor scan flags a "decedent" standing upright. Ronin-7 cuts the boarding party down.
4. The dead troopers' visors already streamed his living face up the chain. An **unnamed handler hologram** (Khall) gives Kessler an ultimatum: deliver "the lost item" within **3 days** or his daughter **Iris** dies.
5. Ronin-7 offers himself in trade; Kessler refuses the bluff. Course set for **Velorum** to take Iris back.

**Ch02 — Velorum, the auction-world**
6. The records broker is a dead end; the only way past him runs through **Resh**, a child-smuggler who knows the back-channels.
7. Ronin-7 **spares Resh** instead of forcing him; the mercy binds him. Resh joins to strike the Dominion at its source.
8. Following the broker's trail, Ronin-7 **frees Iris**, held as collateral. Father and daughter reunite over comm; Iris joins.
9. In the broker's records-vault, Iris finds Ronin-7's own **termination manifest**: his killswitch **fired on his execution date and did not kill him.** *(Killswitch reveal, rung 1.)*
10. Falling back, they find **Mira**, a market child who followed Resh's legend, and lift off.

**Ch03 — aboard The Cairn**
11. Running quiet, the **katana wakes** the instant Ronin-7 grips it: a shadow-AI bonded to his optic feed, heard by him alone (dead steel in any other hand). *(Cage reveal, rung 1.)*
12. It is not a memory chip or ghost but a **defected shadow-AI** that rode his own eyes before the wipe.
13. **Playback:** his last mission on **Kethel-7** — the refusal, and Khall triggering the failed killswitch. *(Conscience reveal, rung 2: he was the first defector.)*
14. Ronin-7 accepts the AI as a witness, not a grave, and **names it Echo.**

**Ch04 — Drovis, the Coil free-port**
15. Chasing Echo's "find the others" charge, the crew tracks Program courier **Tessa Rin** to Drovis. Mira is ordered to stay sealed aboard.
16. An ambient **manhunt** is live (recovery bounty, scan-drones). A tracker, **Mera Voss** (unnamed here), clocks Ronin-7 and slips away.
17. They reach Tessa ahead of a Coil snatch-team. Her data proves the killswitch was **deliberately sabotaged** from inside the Program. *(Killswitch reveal, rung 2.)*
18. At a tapped relay the handler answers and **names himself Khall**, addressing Ronin-7 as **Cipher** — the first time the crew hears the codename. *(Antagonist reveal, rung 1.)*
19. Parkour descent through the flooded **Deepworks** to Kerrax's bolt-hole, Echo calling routes.
20. Ronin-7 beats **Kerrax** and **spares** him — a deliberate choice. **Mera**, watching to confirm the kill, **defects.** *(Conscience reveal, rung 3: the mercy is genuinely his own.)* Act I closes.

**Ch05 — the ash-world**
21. Cross-referencing Velorum files with Mera's Dominion logs surfaces an **ash-world settlement his old line scoured.** Iris flags an unresolved roster name: "Kael Vor."
22. At the mass grave, survivor **Vera Dusk** confronts him with a ledger of the dead, including her fourteen-year-old sister **Kira**. The katana's memory confirms he was there; he names Kira without hesitation.
23. He **dives the memory** of the massacre — his conditioned hands, and a hesitation that came too late.
24. Working the command logs, Iris and Mera expose **"Kael Vor" as a planted Program identity**, never his real name. *(Identity reveal, rung 1.)*
25. Vera demands his life; he offers it. She **refuses** — killing him won't raise Kira, and she sees a weapon that grieves. She releases him to a harder sentence: live, and make it mean something.

**Ch06 — the Iron Dojo, a Dominion mountain citadel**
26. *Hub briefing:* **Resh** names the place his smuggled children were always tagged for — the **Iron Dojo**, a training citadel that *grows* operatives. His one inside contact is a Program engineer, **Morrigan**. Mira is kept sealed aboard; Cipher goes in to break the place that keeps making more of him.
27. The shuttle drops him low in the forest under the air-net; he climbs the mountain the garrison never watches — a **vertical parkour traversal**, Echo calling routes — up to Morrigan's window.
28. **Morrigan** hands him the **kill-list**: three towers, three masters — the Cradle (**Matron Hespa**), the Proving (**Drillmaster Caradoc**), the Vesting (**Master Kaelen**). The rule: kill the cadre, garrison, and machines; never a trainee. The towers unlock in **any order.**
29. Cipher takes the three towers (player-chosen order), each a combat gauntlet ending in the master's death: **Hespa** the gentle erasure-machine, **Caradoc** the brutal forge (boss duel), **Kaelen** the architect (final boss). The children are rescue objectives; one optional mercy is offered only against the Vesting's **vested cadets.**
30. **Kaelen's confession** (gated to fire after all three masters die): the hollowness was **engineered on purpose** ("hollow is the product"), but the seam never fully holds — in the rare ones it opens, and what leaks through is **mercy.** Cipher's flagged "fault" *is* the seam opening, "the prayer that didn't take." *(Conscience reveal, rung 4: the empathy is the designed flaw.)*
31. Cipher returns to **Morrigan**, who triggers a full **evacuation** and brings the citadel down behind them. The eldest trainees walk the youngest down the forest roads (Resh's name opens the doors at the bottom). Morrigan walks out with Cipher and **joins as Ally #3**, naming two unease: a **hand outside the Program** steering it *(seeds the Obsidian Synod, Ch16)* and **one insider/architect** at the root of the work *(seeds Dr. Heris, Ch13).* Cipher names the Hub's training bay after the citadel. The Iron Dojo burns; the hunt widens past the Program.

**Ch07 — a derelict data-reliquary in the dead lanes**
32. *Hub briefing:* **Morrigan's** new analysis bench reads Cipher's sabotaged switch for the first time — every block touched in build order, the work of an **insider** fluent in firmware they shouldn't be. The lead to name them runs through a salvager-archivist who hoards the records of dead operatives: **Coral Vex.** They set a heading for her reliquary, chasing the records and the hand that cut his leash.
33. The Cairn can't dock; Cipher **breaches** the gutted records-barge alone and works inward through the outer stacks — vaulted halls of **mounted blades**, a light in every hilt, each a kept operative's shadow-AI in its steel. **Rival scavengers** pry blades off the racks; the barge's dead **archive-defense automata** still patrol, killing looters and intruder alike. A fight-and-climb gauntlet, the two factions playable against each other.
34. **Trust-test:** Coral watches through the barge's sensors whether he kills or spares the fleeing scavengers. He lets the runners go; at least one crew always escapes regardless, so a witness always lives. *(Spare path earns her warm welcome; a colder variant exists if he hunts every catchable fleer.)*
35. At the tended core he meets **Coral Vex** — an archivist who recognizes him too fast and moves like an operative. The turn: she is not a collector of the dead. She is **one of them**, hiding in plain sight, carrying her own shadow-AI behind her eyes.
36. **Reveal — what came before him:** Coral is of the **Wraith line**, the predecessor variant the Ronin line was refined from, presumed scrapped. She **tore out her own killswitch** by hand, hollowed out a dead salvager's identity, and **kept her own shadow.** Framing is forebear, not sibling — he is her *correction*. She **joins as Ally #4** and berths aboard The Cairn. *(He is not the first of his kind to slip the leash.)*
37. **Reveal — the kept shadows (Cage rung 2):** in the deep archive Coral shows what she guards — erased operatives aren't deleted; their shadow-AIs are **seated in their blades and racked**, every witness shelved and still awake. His sword isn't rare; it's a sample. The Program keeps the blade of every operative it ever killed.
38. **Mini-boss — the berserk blade:** the oldest blade in the hall has gone feral from decades awake. The only thing that reaches a starving shadow is a host-feed, and only an operative survives offering one. Cipher grips it; it bonds to his feed and throws him into a **mindspace duel against the blade's dead previous owner**, whose combat imprint still defends the steel. He beats the imprint; the man, finally allowed to stop, lets go. *(Released, not killed — the victory is the mercy.)*
39. **Ability gained — weakpoint-sight:** the pacified blade's shadow-AI imparts its accumulated combat-read to **Echo**, who now overlays enemy weak points / armor gaps on Cipher's optic feed (first permanent ability unlock). The freed blade is set to rest; Cipher keeps only his katana. Optional **blade-rescue** side-objectives free other coherent kept shadows *(seeds Ch16).*
40. **Reveal — sabotage was dissent (Killswitch rung 3):** at her read bench, with Morrigan analyzing in parallel over comm, Coral reads his switch with the authority of someone who cut her own — the cut was **calm, unhurried, full-authority work**, done by **internal dissent inside the Program.** The saboteur is **not named** *(lands Ch13: Dr. Heris).* Morrigan frames it: an **outside hand** (Ch6) and an **inside crack** (Ch7), two wills pulling at him.
41. **Hook out:** the dissenters scatter where the channels are dead and Coral can't point to them, so she names an older legend instead — the **Silent Garden**, a dead-signal world said to show one seeker one true thing — and roughly where it sits, sending Ronin-7 to see the hand that cut his leash for himself. The crew now hunts the dissenters too. *(Hands into Ch8.)*
42. **Ch8 — Hub briefing:** Coral lays out the legend in full: the **Silent Garden**, an ancient burial world that keeps the **souls** of a people dead millions of years, tended by the **Mourners** (keepers older than the Program, no part of it), who grant one seeker the sight of one hidden truth for the price of a trial that buries more than it answers. No signal reads off the world; it shows on no chart. Echo, for Cipher alone, warns it tests the hand and the head, not the blade. The Cairn sets a heading.
43. **The gate of fog (separation):** an unseen will holds the crew at the gate and admits only Cipher. He enters **alone**, Echo on the blade, comm dead behind him — the first solo walk toward an answer since the Ch3 playback.
44. **The Trial of Mind:** the Mourners set riddles among the graves where the **obedient answer is always wrong** (distrust the issued name; choose the order that indicts him; a leashed thing owes the leash nothing but its end). A false answer rouses **The Buried** — ancient souls he must put down before the puzzle resets — and a flattering lie summons a **Phantom of the Obedient Cipher.** Echo holds silent through the solving. He proves he can think without being told.
45. **The Warden (boss):** the trial passed, the fog raises the **Warden**, a grave-iron-and-fog guardian. Cipher beats it with the **Ch7 weakpoint-sight** (Echo running the read) and it is put back into its grave — settled, not destroyed.
46. **Reveal — the one who defies (Antagonist rung 2):** the Mourners manifest, name him **"the one who defies,"** and open the truth he already carries. Two visions: a **faceless** woman's hands seating the killswitch into a child, whispering *"I hope you will save us"* (seen, **not named** — lands Ch13); and **Khall** at the moment of the switch, agreeing Cipher was right, triggering it anyway, grieving it (*"I'm sorry it has come to this"*), and beginning to doubt the order (*"who forges a leash"*). *(Antagonist ladder rung 2 — Khall's recorded guilt; the forged-order twist is not disclosed.)*
47. **Hook out:** Cipher carries out a truth he can't fully share; Morrigan frames the Ch6 outside hand + the Ch7 inside crack + the Garden's visions as a war happening *inside* the Program, not a wall. The Mourners give no peace, only the road — the answers aren't on the surface; **go down**, into the Program's buried infrastructure. *(Closes Act II; hands into Ch9.)*
48. **Ch9 — Hub briefing (Act III opens):** the war-room holo-table awake for the first time, the roster crowded and loud. Morrigan pins a buried Program **relay** under the **Rustfang**, a pirate free-hold dug into a dead world's caves and held by the old warrior **Gryph**; the signal is older and bigger than a comms node should be, and it feeds something deeper. Coral warns the deep is where the Program hides the kept; Resh marks Gryph as a man you cannot buy, only earn. The trail goes *down*, into water. The Cairn deploys.
49. **The descent (Rustfang hold):** Cipher works down alone through the vertical pirate hold, the crew on comm thinning with depth, Echo calling the drops. The deeper he goes, the older the machinery: the band's fortifications give way to sealed, humming **Program-original** structures the crew built down onto for years without understanding. Not a base — **infrastructure.** They dug their hold into the lid of a buried worksite.
50. **Gryph's bargain (protector choice / Ally #5):** at the deep overlook, **Coil raiders** storm the shelf for the same buried prize. Cipher fights the raid **side by side** with Gryph and his band instead of taking the map off a corpse — Act III's thesis made literal. The old warrior, who has only ever watched outsiders come to *take* the hold, gives his map, his knowledge of the deep, and his sword warrior to warrior. **Ally #5**, on his own hard terms.
51. **The Tide depths — the older make (boss):** below the hold, a flooded archive-trench where the racks of Ch7 are wired into a living machine and carrying current. Sable (a living archive, see beat 53) — her node packed with the **Wraith** generation's shadows — is kept by **Vane / Wraith-6**, a survivor of the Wraith program (the make right before Ronin, Coral's make) whose **leash never broke** — not Cipher's twin but his forebear, an older make of the same cage. He cannot be reasoned with or spared; to free Sable, Cipher must put him down. The chapter's sole boss, a duel against an older form of his own technique in black water. *(The literal dark-mirror — a leashed copy of Cipher himself — waits for Ch12.)*
52. **Ability gained — Overdrive:** on Vane's death his freed **blade-shadow** floods into Echo, who spikes it through the optic feed — the world stops, Cipher moving full-speed through frozen water and frozen guns. **Overdrive**, a chargeable time-dilation burst (second permanent ability after Ch7's weakpoint-sight; the two stack). Framed as the only mercy the Dominion ever left him.
53. **Sable freed (Ally #6):** with the keeper dead, Cipher frees **Sable** from the lattice — an operative racked with dozens of kept shadow-AIs, conscious through all of them, the crew's living map of the buried machine. She asks to be wanted as a person, not collected as wire. **Ally #6.**
54. **Reveal — the Concord Engine (Killswitch rung 4 + Cage rung 3):** Sable and Morrigan map what the relay feeds. The killswitch is only a per-operative leash; the same suppression science is scaled into the **Concord Engine**, a galaxy-spanning broadcast weapon to switch off the will to refuse world by world **(Killswitch rung 4).** Worse, the Engine *runs on the kept* — the living archives conscripted as its transmission lattice, every shelved shadow a forced relay **(Cage rung 3).**
55. **The hold kept + hook out:** climbing out, Gryph appoints his second **Rook** keeper of the Rustfang hold — the band stays, the cave holds, the old warrior leaves with the crew. To unmake the Engine they need its build-record, and it was never one book — the Dominion split it across living archives like Sable, scattered across the galaxy. Sable is the map to them; the first is filed in the **Ninefold mines.** *(Opens Act III; hands into Ch10.)*

56. **Ch10 — Hub briefing:** Morrigan lays out the gap the Concord Engine reveal opened — the build-record is split across **living archives** like Sable, each carrying one section. Sable can feel the nearest node, filed deep in the **Ninefold mine shafts** (nine descending tiers where no signal reaches), and tells Cipher privately it's the first of three she can feel and the only one she thinks they bring up alive. Gryph, who knows deep-shaft work, throws in. Echo, for Cipher alone, wonders whose names they're about to read — maybe even his own.
57. **The Ninefold descent:** Cipher works down the nine-tier shaft-complex, each tier older and stranger, through mine automata and escalating environmental hazards (collapsing shafts, lifts) to the record-store the Dominion keeps physically, deep where no signal reaches — paranoia made architecture.
58. **Cassie-04 & her keeper (Ally #7 / boss):** in shaft seven hangs **Cassie-04**, a living archive racked with the **Ninja** generation's kept shadows, her lattice carrying the **ledger of the erased** — every deleted operative's true name and disposal site, plus the cross-index to the other two nodes. Her keeper is **Sever / Ninja-2**, a still-leashed survivor of the Ninja program (two makes before Ronin) — the Vane of this descent, but *almost* present, a man straining against his own leash. He can't be reasoned with; to free Cassie, Cipher puts him down. Sable, watching a sister come off the rack instead of dying on it, convinces Cassie she can be more than infrastructure. **Ally #7.**
59. **Ability gained — Phase-step:** Sever's freed **blade-shadow** floods into Echo and unlocks **Phase-step**, a short blink through matter and bodies (third permanent ability; chain = weakpoint-sight → Overdrive → Phase-step).
60. **Vess's vengeance (Ally #8 / the mercy-mirror):** as the crew climbs out, **Vess** — a raider whose people Ronin's old line destroyed — takes him at the ship entrance, blade to his neck. The names Cassie carries change everything: killing him buries the evidence, sparing him weaponizes the ledger against the machine. **Vess spares him**, reverses her grip, and joins as a lethal protector who won't let him die until every name is spoken — the protagonist's own mercy mirrored back. **Ally #8.**
61. **Reveal — the ledger operationalized (Ladder E):** no new rung; Ch10 turns the scattered living-archive nodes into the usable **ledger of the erased** and pays off Ch5's ledger motif. Cassie's cross-index, laid over the Engine schematic, lights the **two remaining nodes** — a leviathan **bone-canyon** (far older, reads *wrong*) and a Dominion **cryo-command vault.** Predators have become protectors with a *plan.* *(Hands into Ch11.)*

62. **Ch11 — Hub briefing:** Cassie overlays her lattice on the Engine schematic and one node pulses out of place — an ancient **arkship** fossilized in a leviathan **bone-canyon**, older than the Dominion, that the records tie to the Program's very origin. It reads *wrong*: Sable names it a sister so long racked her kept shadows have begun leaking outward — one they may not bring up alive — and warns the canyon's "atmosphere" is the node's broadcast, showing you your dead. Vess wants to see where the thing that took her people began; Echo, for Cipher alone, is wary of something older than the leash itself.
63. **The bone-canyon & narcosis dreamscape:** the crew traverses the leviathan skeleton and the half-fossilized arkship. The broken node's leaking shadows are the **Knight** generation (the Program's first make, the most overloaded), manifesting as a waking **narcosis dreamscape** — Cipher walks his ghosts: Kira (Ch5), the Kethel-7 dead (Ch3), the ledger faces, and a recurring **younger self** (no augments, no scars) who insists there was someone before the sword and the number, and won't give his name. *(Seeds Soren, Ch16.)*
64. **Aldric / Knight-1 (boss):** the keeper is **Aldric / Knight-1**, the eldest operative there is — the very first of the very first program, the make all later makes were cut from — half-lost to the broadcast, fought *through* the dream as the dead keep interrupting, wielding the oldest, heaviest, most archaic school the player faces. Nothing left in him to free, only to quiet; Cipher puts him down. But killing the keeper does **not** clear the dream — the dreaming is the archive's own.
65. **Ability gained — Unbroken:** Aldric's freed **blade-shadow** floods into Echo and unlocks **Unbroken**, a mind-ward granting narcosis/fear resistance and surviving one otherwise-lethal blow (fourth permanent ability). Now immune to the broadcast, Cipher walks the still-active dream alone, comm cut, Echo the only voice, to the arkship's origin core.
66. **Reveal — the origin / make-lineage (Ladder C, rung 2 setup):** read by **Echo alone** (comm down), the oldest section of the build-record is the origin — the Program began by taking a small set of **template operatives** and **scattering** their clones across the galaxy, run forward in generations: **Knight → Ninja → Wraith → Ronin**, each make refined from the last, each shelved into a node when superseded. Vane, Sever, and Aldric were the last leashed survivors of the three makes before Ronin's. Cipher takes it as proof he is the *latest copy* of a scattered template — whose original face he wears is the question he carries out. *(Inverts in Ch12: not the newest copy, the **prime template** the editions were cloned from.)*
67. **The second kill + homecoming hook:** the record read, Cipher turns to the dreamer who kept it — a sister too far gone to bring up. He gives her the same door he gave the keeper and **puts her down clean.** Her death ends the broadcast, collapses the dream, and floods the **comm back.** A brief grief: Sable feels her go, and **Coral recognizes Wraith as her own make** off the recovered fragment. Cassie calls them home: two nodes down, one to go — the last archive is in a Dominion **cryo-command vault**, and a rival force is already racing for it. And if the template was scattered, *whose* face is his? *(Hands into Ch12.)*

68. **Ch12 — Hub briefing (Act III climax):** the arkship's origin data (Ch11) and Sable's map converge on a Dominion **cryo-command vault** where the **last scattered archive** is wired into the operative **command-network** as its living interface, with a dormant authority — **Commander Vale** — in stasis as the command-key. Cassie's cross-index reads what this node holds and stops the room cold: where Ch9–11 held the Wraith, Ninja, and Knight makes, this one holds the **Ronin** generation — his own make. A rival force is already racing for it. The roster splits — Morrigan and Coral wary of touching a command-network at all, Vess and Mera pushing for speed, Kessler asking what such a tool does to whoever picks it up, Sable steeling herself to reach the last of her kind. The argument *is* the chapter's thesis: this crew refuses easy power. They deploy in a race for the vault.
69. **The descent & waking Vale:** the crew (and the rival force) race down to the last node, which hangs wired into the network with **Vale** asleep beside it as its key — whoever holds both could command the scattered legions.
70. **Vale's promise:** Vale wakes and sees in Cipher not a defect but an *opportunity* — the whole "**defective**" generation can be woken into a self-aware **army** he means to *lead*, to conquer, not free. To keep Cipher off the node he loops in the network's nearest leashed asset: **a clone of Ronin-7 himself**, woken and conditioned as his enforcer.
71. **The gut-punch (Identity rung 2 / boss):** the network readout names him plainly — Ronin-7 is not the newest copy but the **template**: the seventh Ronin and the first that held, **1 through 6 flawed and culled**, and every edition on the network was cloned **from him.** The enforcer wears his own face because that face is *his.* All Act III he put down *older* makes (Vane the Wraith, Sever the Ninja, Aldric the Knight); this last keeper is the **Ronin** make — it is him, and it came out of him. He cuts down his own copy to reach the node, **inverting** the Ch11 "you're a copy" supposition into source-horror. *(Identity ladder, rung 2 — he is the prime template.)*
72. **Ability gained — Mirror:** the edition's freed **blade-shadow** floods into Echo and unlocks **Mirror**, the power to summon a phantom double of himself in battle (fifth permanent ability; chain = weakpoint-sight → Overdrive → Phase-step → Unbroken → Mirror). The clone reveal made into a weapon.
73. **Refusing the throne + hook out:** Vale offers Cipher the head of the army; Cipher refuses — an army of the freed, *commanded,* is just the cage with a new warden. He **cuts the last node loose** from the command-network and takes its fragment; with it the Engine's **build-record is finally whole** — every node, every conscript, the order of the build. Vale survives as a rival vision for what the awakened should become. To *use* the build-record — to unmake the Engine and free the kept — Cipher must face the maker who built him and the leash. *(Closes Act III; hands into Ch13.)*

74. **Ch13 — the sensing (Act IV opens):** the moment Cipher steps back aboard *The Cairn* with the last fragment, **Cassie** and **Sable** go still at once. They *feel* it: out past the lattice they spent the war tracing is **one archive larger than all four nodes combined** — the four scattered nodes were never the machine, only its veins. This is the trunk: not a build-record, not a graveyard of shed shadows, but a **transmitter** — the **Concord Engine** itself, racked and waiting, scaled to do to whole worlds what a killswitch does to one operative. Sable can feel its weight and can't feel a seam anywhere on it.
75. **The convergence on Heris:** the room reckons with the size of it. **Morrigan** gives the answer — you don't break a leash this big by force, you find the one person who built it and left a flaw. Whoever planted the gap in Cipher's killswitch did it from *inside* the suppression science (the dissent thread she has tracked since the dojo, Ch7), and that same insider hand is the only mind that could know where a leash this size is soft. **Find the saboteur, find the Engine's seam.** The sensing converges with the trails the crew already had — the command-network (Ch12) and the internal-dissent traffic (Ch7) both terminate at one sterile lab: that of **Dr. Heris**, the Program's own architect. The two hunts are one.
76. **The sterile vault:** after caves and bone and rust, a place of unbearable cleanliness — Heris's white laboratory-sanctum, where people were *designed.* Cipher breaches it through lab security and loyalist constructs; a **Program enforcer** sent to silence Heris (mini-boss) proves she is now a target too, not a trap.
77. **The maker (Killswitch rung 5 — closes Ladder A):** Heris admits everything. She **built Ronin-7** and **built his killswitch** — and **planted the flaw** in his suppression that let his conscience fracture and the switch fail (the sabotage of Ch4, the internal dissent of Ch7 — it was *her*). And she names the larger sin: the Dominion conscripted her under threat to scale her suppression science into the galaxy-silencing **Concord Engine** (Ch9), and she seeded the same kind of deniable flaw into *it* too — a seam she has held the location of for years, waiting for the man she broke open to come back. The hand that made the leash also made the gap, at both sizes. She is the **faceless woman** of the Ch8 Garden vision, now named. *(Killswitch ladder, rung 5 — the saboteur named; the ladder closes.)*
78. **Heris defects (Ally #9):** she asks no forgiveness, only *work* — to finish what she designed into him by helping tear the Engine apart through the hidden seam. **Ally #9.** The crew gains **Engine-sabotage tech**; the architect of the cage becomes the engineer of its destruction.

He does not take the throne; he leaves it in **ashes.** The galaxy of freed killers chooses, for the first time, what to be. *(Closes Act IV and the story: mercy was the rebellion; freely-made choice is the victory.)*
