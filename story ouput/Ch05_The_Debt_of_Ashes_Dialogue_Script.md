SETTING / ENVIRONMENT — WHERE THIS CHAPTER TAKES PLACE
Reference for art, lighting, level, and audio direction. Chapter 4 was a city
that turned itself into a net to sell him; Chapter 5 is the bill that city's net
was hiding. Act II opens by turning the empowerment of Act I around to face its
cost. The whole chapter is built on a single, terrible fact already established
in canon: the man the crew follows was, before the wipe, the Program's clean
hand — and the katana on his hip kept the recording of every clean thing it did.
This chapter takes him to one of those recordings and makes him stand inside it.

The chapter unfolds across an ASH-WORLD: a dead settlement on a grey planet
scoured by his old operative line, where the soil is ash to the ankle and the wind
carries it in slow grey curtains. There is no syndicate here, no garrison, no
bounty. The antagonist is not a person to fight but GRIEF, given a face by a
survivor named VERA DUSK. Treat the place as a wound, not a level: traversal and
horror over spectacle, scavenger threats only, no boss. The tone is elegiac — ash,
weight, the quiet of a place where everything loud already happened.
(Full ship layout: 17_THE_HUB_Kesslers_Ship.md.)

The chapter moves through five guided segments, descending from the warm light of
the ship into the cold of a grave and back out under a harder sentence.

  SEGMENT 0 — THE CAIRN (the briefing, before the drop)
  - Not the ash-world: Kessler's ship, the command room, more of its dead bridge
    lit than a chapter ago. The salvaged holo-table now throws up two overlaid
    record-sets: Velorum's termination files and Mera Voss's old Dominion
    operation-logs, cross-referenced into a single hard lead. The crew is larger
    now and one of them is new and cold to the touch. This is the quiet before a
    job the crew chooses with open eyes, knowing it will cost them. Where the
    course is set, Mira is kept close, and the ash-world becomes a heading.

  SEGMENT 1 — THE SETTLEMENT (ash-world surface / the approach to the grave)
  - A scoured settlement under a grey sky: collapsed prefab shells, a market square
    gone to ash, doors standing open on rooms no one came back to. Wind, ash-drift,
    the creak of dead structures. The crew comes down into a silence that has a
    shape to it. Light scavenger presence picking the bones of the place.
  - Where VERA DUSK steps out of the ash with a ledger of the dead and a drawn
    weapon, and the chapter stops being a mission and becomes an accounting.

  SEGMENT 2 — THE MEMORY-DIVE (inside the katana's recording of the massacre)
  - Not a place that still exists: the settlement as it was, the day it died,
    replayed off the katana's stored memory. Desaturated, wrong, edges that smear
    like a signal under strain. The same square, alive and then not. A horror-tone
    set-piece, limited combat — the player moves through a memory he cannot change.
    Echo walks it with him, the only other thing awake inside the recording.

  SEGMENT 3 — THE LEDGER (records cross-check, relayed to the grave) — REVEAL
  - Aboard the Cairn, Iris and Mera work the operation's command records against
    his own Program file the whole time the landing party is below, and relay the
    result down over comm. A small, still scene of two data-sets refusing to agree.
    Where the name "Kael Vor" comes apart in her hands. (Reveal Ladder C, rung 1.)

  SEGMENT 4 — THE RECKONING (graveside)
  - The grave itself, at the chapter's end: a long low scar in the ash with the
    ledger laid on it. Vera, Cipher, and a drawn weapon between them. Where a life
    is offered and refused, and a harder sentence is passed. The crew watches from
    a respectful distance; Vera does not come with them. The wind, the ash, and one
    survivor releasing the man who took her sister into a debt he has to outlive.

INTRUDING / RECURRING ELEMENTS
- THE ASH (the place itself): the planet's scoured surface as a constant presence —
  underfoot, in the air, settling on everything. It is the chapter's atmosphere and
  carries its title. Treat it as weather and as accusation: the dead are not buried
  here so much as spread thin over everything the crew touches.
- THE LEDGER OF THE DEAD: Vera's record of the lost, ID-chits and names. The motif
  this chapter plants (the full mechanic lands Ch10). Every name in it is a thing
  the crew cannot give back. It is the chapter's only weapon, and Vera carries it.
- SCAVENGERS: the only live threat on the ash-world. Pickers stripping a dead town,
  not soldiers, not a cause. Light, optional, atmospheric. They scatter from grief
  the same as anyone. They are not what the chapter is about and the script treats
  them as texture, not enemy.
- ECHO (the katana's shadow-AI): heard by Ronin-7 alone, wired to his optic feed,
  sees what he sees. Calls him Cipher. The wry warmth that carried Ch4 drops away
  here; in the grave and in the memory-dive Echo is only the witness, steady and
  low. The one voice that was actually there with him the first time.

TONE OF PLACE
Chapter 4 was a descent out of noise into a silence where he chose to spare a man.
Chapter 5 is the silence that was always underneath that choice. Act I asked what
it costs a weapon to spare. Act II opens by asking what it cost the people it did
not spare — before the fracture, before the mercy, when the hand was still clean
and obedient. There is no win condition here the player can force; the dead stay
dead, the name in the record is a lie, and the only thing the chapter hands him
at the end is a heavier reason to keep living. It is the saga's first full breath
of the cost. Ash, grief, weight. The empowerment of Act I, sent the bill.
