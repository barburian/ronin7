SETTING / ENVIRONMENT — WHERE THIS CHAPTER TAKES PLACE
Reference for art, lighting, level, and audio direction. Chapter 7 took him to
the place that keeps the proof, and a forebear at his shoulder read his switch and
named the hand that cut it: internal dissent, a crack inside the machine. She could
not point him to the dissenters themselves, but she could name an older rumor, a
place that grants one seeker the sight of one true thing. Chapter 8 is that place.
It is the supernatural climax of Act II, and it is where the act stops resting.
The chapter unfolds in the SILENT GARDEN, a fog-drowned burial world that answers
no comms and sits on no chart: a horizonless plain of headstones running off into
grey in every direction, tended by the MOURNERS, robed keepers older than any power
now living, who keep the souls of a people that lived and died on this world ages
before the Program was ever dreamed. There is no sky.
There is no sound but the one Ronin-7 brings with him. Where Chapter 7 was a tomb
that someone tends, this is a graveyard that thinks, and the thing it does to the
people who enter it is not violence first. It is examination. It buries more
seekers than it answers, and it answers exactly one truth to exactly the right
kind of seeker.

The Garden is cold the way a held breath is cold. Fog stands waist-high over the
grave-rows and never moves unless the place wants it to. Light has no source; it
is just present, flat and grey, the light of an overcast with no cloud above it.
Sound design is the inverse of Chapter 7's undertone: where the reliquary was a
chorus that never went quiet, the Garden is a silence so total it has pressure,
broken only by Ronin-7's own footfalls, his own breath, and the MOURNERS' voice,
which arrives without a mouth and from no direction. Treat that silence as the
chapter's pulse. The horror here is not that the dead are kept awake. It is that
the dead are listening, and they will rise to correct a lie.

The chapter moves through six guided beats. After the mandatory Hub briefing it
becomes the most solitary level since Chapter 3: the crew is held at the gate and
cannot follow, and for the only time in Act II the player walks toward an answer
with no one at his back but the shadow in his sword. The core loop is a puzzle
gauntlet of the mind, punctuated by spectral combat on failure, climaxing in a
single boss and a single truth.

  SEGMENT 0 — THE CAIRN (the briefing, before the heading)
  - Not the Garden: Kessler's ship, the command room, the whole crew assembled.
    Coral Vex stands over a chart with a blank where a world should be. She named
    the Silent Garden at the close of Chapter 7 and she knows the legend better
    than anyone alive: a burial world tended by keepers older than the Program,
    who hold the souls of a people long dead and grant one seeker the sight of one
    hidden truth, for the price of a trial that has buried more than it has answered. They set a heading for a world that
    answers no comms and shows on no chart. Echo, for Cipher alone, warns that this
    place tests the hand and the head, not the blade.

  SEGMENT 1 — THE GATE OF FOG (the outer grounds) — TRAVERSAL + SEPARATION
  - The Cairn sets down on a world of low fog and endless headstones. An unseen
    will admits only the one it means to test; the crew is held at the gate as
    surely as a wall, and Ronin-7 goes in alone, Echo riding the blade at his hip.
    The first time since Chapter 3 he walks toward an answer with no one at his
    back. The Garden wanted him alone, which means the Garden already knows him.

  SEGMENT 2 — THE TRIAL OF MIND (the grave-paths) — PUZZLE GAUNTLET
  - The MOURNERS speak without appearing, a single cold voice braided from many.
    They set him riddles among the graves: trials of memory, of truth, of will.
    Solve true and the fog opens a path. Choose false and the dead rise to answer
    for him, spectral guardians he must put down before the trial will let him try
    again. The deeper he goes, the less the puzzles test what he knows and the more
    they test what he refuses to admit. The Program trained him to obey without
    thinking; the Garden will not let him pass until he can think without being
    told. (Three seed puzzles framed in production notes; not over-designed here.)

  SEGMENT 3 — THE WARDEN (the deep garden, the oldest grave) — BOSS
  - The last riddle answered true, the fog draws back from a single ancient barrow,
    and the Garden raises its final test: the WARDEN, a towering guardian of
    grave-iron and fog wearing the shape of the oldest thing buried here. The mind
    is proven; now the Garden takes the body. Ronin-7 fights it with the Chapter 7
    weakpoint-sight, Echo running the read, and puts the Warden back in its grave.

  SEGMENT 4 — THE ONE WHO DEFIES (the still center) — REVEAL
  - The MOURNERS finally manifest, ringing the barrow, and name him: the one who
    defies. Not the strongest seeker, not the cleverest; the one the leash could
    not hold. To such a one the Garden owes its single truth. The fog becomes a
    window into two things he has never been allowed to look at: the faceless hand
    that seated the killswitch in a child's skull, and Khall, in the moment of the
    switch, grieving and beginning to doubt the order. Both seen, neither named.

  SEGMENT 5 — THE LEAVING (the gate of fog) — EXIT + HOOK
  - The Garden opens. Ronin-7 walks back out to a crew who waited at a gate they
    could not cross, carrying a truth he cannot fully share and a face he could not
    see. The Mourners do not give him peace. They name what he is and let him go.
    The woman's plea and Khall's doubt both point the same way: down, into the
    Program's buried infrastructure. The trail descends into the pits and deeps of
    Act III. This chapter closes Act II.

INTRUDING / RECURRING ELEMENTS
- THE FOG (the will of the place): waist-high, motionless, sourceless. It is the
  Garden's hand. It holds the crew at the gate, it opens paths when he answers
  true, it draws back to raise the Warden, and at the reveal it becomes a window
  into the two visions. Treat it as an active character, never as weather. When it
  moves, the Garden has decided something.
- THE SILENCE (the chapter's pulse): the deliberate inverse of Chapter 7's chorus.
  Total, pressurized, broken only by Ronin-7's own body and the Mourners' voice.
  When the dead rise on a wrong answer, the silence does not break with sound; it
  breaks with the headstones, which is worse. Let the player feel watched by quiet.
- THE MOURNERS (the keepers): robed figures older than any power now living, who
  keep the souls of this world's vanished people and never appear until the reveal.
  What they are, and how a soul is kept, is left unexplained by design; they are no
  part of the Program and hold none of its dead. Their voice is a single cold thing
  braided from many, arriving from no direction and no mouth. Not cruel. Not kind.
  Implacable and ancient, examiners with no stake in whether he lives. They speak as
  "we." They call him only "the one who defies," never Cipher, never the serial.
- ECHO (the katana's shadow-AI): heard by Ronin-7 alone, wired to his optic feed,
  sees what he sees. Calls him Cipher. In Chapter 7 Echo stood at bare witness in a
  hall of its own kind. Here Echo is the only ally that crosses the gate, and it
  knows the limits of its own use: this is not a place a blade or a combat-read can
  save him from. Echo runs the weakpoint-sight when the Warden comes, but for the
  trial it can do nothing but stay with him and refuse to answer for him, because
  the Garden has forbidden it and because answering for him is the one thing that
  would get him buried. The witness, this once, must let the man think alone.
- THE BURIED (spectral guardians): the ancient dead of this world, the souls the
  Garden keeps, roused to correct a lie. Not persistent monsters; consequences.
  They rise from the graves the wrong answer touched, must be put down, and sink
  again when the disturbance settles, at which point the failed puzzle resets for
  another attempt. They are grievable, not evil. They are doing their job, and they
  are older than every war the living are still fighting.

TONE OF PLACE
Chapter 7 answered what happened to all the others and ended on the first proof
that the Program is fighting itself, a forebear at his shoulder and a quiet place
named down the dead channels. Chapter 8 walks into that quiet place alone and
finds it is not a sanctuary at all. It is a test, older than the thing that made
him, run by keepers who owe him nothing and will give him everything if he can
prove he is the one rumor said could not be leashed. The exhale of Act II is gone.
This is the act's held breath and its dread. The arc moves from a Hub full of
crew to a gate that takes only one, through a gauntlet that hunts the mind harder
than any garrison hunted the body, to a single ancient grave and a single owed
truth, and lands on two visions stacked on each other: a woman whose face he is
not allowed to see prayed he would survive the thing she put in him, and the man
who pulled his leash grieves it and has begun to doubt the order that loosed him.
He came for the hand that broke his switch. He leaves with two ghosts, a name he
does not yet have, and a title the Garden will not let him put down. He is the one
who defies, and the trail he is owed runs straight down, into the deeps.

BEAT 2 — THE TRIAL OF MIND (The Grave-Paths — Puzzle Gauntlet)

[Interior to the Garden: the grave-paths proper, the chapter's puzzle core. The fog parts only as far ahead as the current puzzle, so the player advances pocket by pocket of revealed ground, each a small set-piece riddle posed by the Mourners. Solve true and the fog opens the way on. Solve false and the buried rise. The three seed puzzles below are gestured as placeholders, framed in production notes, not fully mechanized here; the dialogue carries the theme, which is constant: the obedient answer, the answer the Program trained into him, is always the wrong one. Echo holds its forced silence except where noted, and the strain of holding it is part of the scene. Ronin-7 speaks his reasoning aloud, sparely, the way a man does when the only one who can hear him has been told not to help.]

[PRODUCTION NOTE — THE DISTURBANCE LOOP (objective 2): the trial is governed by a "disturbance" state, not a health bar. Each puzzle accepts a player input (a step, a placement, a spoken-or-selected answer). A TRUE input opens the fog and advances. A FALSE input wakes THE BURIED: spectral guardians rise from the specific graves the lie touched, scaled to the depth reached, and must be put down in a contained wave. Surviving the wave settles the disturbance and RESETS THE SAME PUZZLE for another attempt, with the previously-tried false inputs now visibly disturbed ground (soft fail-forward, no progress lost but no progress gained). The combat is the cost of a wrong answer, never the goal; the puzzle is never brute-forced by fighting. Tune wave size and puzzle count in systems design. Critically: the buried are grievable, not monstrous, the encounter audio reads as sorrow, and a put-down guardian sinks back into its grave rather than dying a second death.]

[PRODUCTION NOTE — THE BURIED (spectral guardian waves): non-persistent enemies raised by puzzle failure. Visual: grave-grey, half-fog, wearing the worn shape of whoever the lie slandered, armed with whatever they died holding. They do not taunt and they do not speak; only the Mourners speak. They scale with trial depth (early waves are few and slow; the wave after the third puzzle's failure is the hardest non-boss fight of the chapter). Weakpoint-sight (Ch7) works on them, and Echo MAY run the read during a wave because combat is allowed help, the trial is not; this is the one place Echo's barks return mid-trial. They sink, never gib. Author the full bark pool once geometry exists; samples in the Echo production note below.]

[PRODUCTION NOTE — ECHO IN-TRIAL CONDUCT: Echo is mechanically and narratively SILENT during puzzle-solving itself (the Garden forbids assistance, and assistance gets Cipher buried; this is established in BEAT 1 and must be felt as restraint, not absence). Echo's voice returns in exactly two registers: (a) during a spectral wave, where combat help is permitted, Echo runs threat calls and weakpoint reads like any fight; (b) between puzzles, in short pressure-valve lines that comment on the toll WITHOUT solving the next riddle. Author the bark pools once level geometry is built. Sample wave pool: "Reads up, Cipher, three rising on your left, old soldiers, put them down gentle and fast." "There's the seam, low on the spear-arm, same as the racks." "It doesn't want to fight you. Doesn't change what you have to do. Settle it." Sample between-puzzle pool: "I'm right here and I'm not saying a word. Hate it. Keep going." "You felt that, didn't you. The easy answer pulling at you. That's the leash. Don't take its hand." "Whatever they ask next, ask yourself who taught you the quick answer." All Echo lines are heard by Cipher alone, off the optic feed.]

[The first pocket of fog opens. A short causeway of flat headstones laid into the ground like steppingstones crosses a low black water, and each stone bears a name. THE MOURNERS' voice settles over it.]

[PRODUCTION NOTE — PUZZLE A: THE GRAVE OF TRUE NAMES (seed, placeholder). A path of laid headstones the player must cross by stepping only on the TRUE names of the ancient dead, the names this world's vanished people called themselves, while FALSE names, the ones later hands carved over the graves (scavengers, mapmakers, conquerors who renamed a dead they never knew), are unsafe and wake the slandered sleepers. The teaching content: the false names are the clean, official, freshly-cut ones; the true names are worn and strange. Cipher should recognize the species of lie, an imposed name laid over a real one, as the same kind of fiction once laid over him, so the solve requires him to distrust a name precisely because it looks official and new. Final stone-set and name-list designed in a later pass; the dialogue below carries the lesson, not the layout.]
