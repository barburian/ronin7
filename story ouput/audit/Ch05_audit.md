## 1. Header

**Overall naturalness grade: C+**

The prose is atmospheric, well-controlled, and contraction-rich (no missing-contraction problem). But it is dominated by two faults that recur in nearly every major line: (a) aphorism-stacking / over-balanced "quotable maxim" phrasing, and (b) "not X, it's Y" antithesis constructions. Almost every character speaks in the same elevated, sententious register, so the smuggler, the captain, the tracker, the survivor, and the AI are hard to tell apart by voice. It passes the one hard rule (zero em-dashes in speech) cleanly.

### Systemic naturalness finding — register uniformity
Resh (clipped smuggler), Kessler (gravelly Terran), Mera (flat tracker), Vera (raw survivor), Ronin-7, and Echo all deliver the same kind of balanced, sententious maxim. Examples that should read as *different mouths* but don't: Resh's "We don't leave people standing alone in a place like this" (Beat 4) is as polished as Ronin's aphorisms; Kessler's tricolon (Beat 0) is a writer's cadence, not a 50s salvager's. Recommend pulling the secondary cast (Resh, Kessler, Mera) toward plainer, rougher, more interruptible speech and reserving the elegiac maxim-register for the two leads of the scene (Vera and Ronin-7), and even there thinning it so the big lines land harder by contrast.

## 4. Prose summary — biggest recurring problems

Ch05 is tonally assured and clean on the project's one hard rule: there is not a single em-dash in any spoken line. Its weakness is uniform polish. The chapter is built almost entirely out of quotable maxims and "it's not X, it's Y" reversals, and they are distributed evenly across every speaker, so the cast loses individuality. The two leads of the scene (Vera and Ronin-7) carry the elegiac, aphoristic register well, but it bleeds into Resh, Kessler, and Mera, who should sound rougher and more interruptible. The single most over-written moment is Vera's Beat 4 "A weapon that grieves. That's the thing they can't allow," which states the chapter's thesis outright (the voice note even admits this) and would hit harder implied than declared. The fix is subtractive, not additive: thin the maxims, break a few sentences into false starts and fragments for the secondary cast, and let the listener supply the subtext the dialogue currently spells out. Canon consistency is solid: codenames, the Hub briefing structure, the shadow-AI rules, the "Kael Vor" reveal, the crew split, and the internal timeline all hold with zero violations.
