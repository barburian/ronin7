# Ch13 — "The Sterile Reckoning" — Dialogue Audit

**Chapter:** RONIN-7, Chapter 13: The Sterile Reckoning (Dialogue + Voice Direction Script)
**Scope:** AUDIT ONLY. No script edited.
**Overall naturalness grade: C-**

The hard em-dash ban PASSES completely (zero em-dashes in any `Line:`). Contractions are used everywhere, and there are genuine false starts ("I've been trying not to say this since the second ward and I can't hold it anymore"). But the chapter is dominated by one AI-tell rhetorical move — the **"not X, it's Y" antithesis** — appearing in nearly every speaker and nearly every beat, compounded by aphorism-stacking, a homogenized "everyone is a poet" register, several true monologue-length speeches the production notes explicitly forbid ("never a monologue / never a dump"), and exposition delivered to people who already lived it. That sameness is what drags an otherwise emotionally legible script down to C-.

## 4. Summary — biggest recurring problems

1. **The "not X, it's Y" antithesis is the chapter's load-bearing rhythm, and it's the loudest AI tell.** It appears ~22 times across *every* speaker — the dry clerk Cassie, the blunt engineer Morrigan, the cold surgeon Heris, the protagonist, and Echo all reach for the same construction. It is so consistent that it erases the characters' distinct registers: strip the speaker labels and most lines are interchangeable. This is the single change that would most raise the grade — let only one or two voices (Heris's clinical precision earns it) use balanced antithesis, and force everyone else into rougher, less symmetrical speech.

2. **Aphorism-stacking and "poet's ending."** Almost every speech closes on a quotable maxim ("clean is how you pretend it isn't what it is," "she is the flaw," "you'll have one and never the other," "She built us a place to put the dead"). Real dialogue rarely lands a perfect epigram every turn; the relentlessness reads as authored, not spoken.

3. **Monologue length vs. the chapter's own brief, plus exposition to people who already know.** The production notes repeatedly demand "never a monologue, never a dump," yet the chapter carries nine 40-56s single speeches, several of which re-explain established facts (the nodes-as-veins, the Ch8 dream re-narrated to the man who lived it, the canon-correction lecture). Cutting these in half and converting them to true exchanges would honor the script's own stated intent.

Secondary: a cluster of cross-character pet phrases ("...for the sky," "held breath," "the only X in the only Y," "crossed a galaxy") that should be assigned to single owners. On consistency the chapter is strong — only one real defect (the Ch8 whisper is described in Heris's voice note but missing from her actual `Line:`, with a mismatched 57s duration), plus one soft framing overstatement in the exit hook.
