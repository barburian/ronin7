### 2D. Aphorism-stacking / maxim register (every line quotable)

Flagged because it removes naturalism — real speech isn't this epigrammatic, and it homogenizes all speakers:
- Beat 0 Ronin (L212): "the Program does its work where it thinks no one will follow. So someone follows."
- Beat 1 Coral (L266): "A relay needs a wall. A wall needs a worksite."
- Beat 1 Coral (L266): "It gets worse before it gets to the bottom. It always does."
- Beat 2 Ronin (L335): "I'm done being the thing that takes."
- Beat 3 Vane (L382): "They send us where no one follows. I am where they sent me."
- Beat 3 Echo (L392): "There's no one in there to talk to. Only the order."
- Beat 4 Ronin (L502): "we take this whole machine apart, node by node, until every shadow strung into it is loose."

These don't all need cutting, but the *density* is the problem: trim so maxims land once or twice per beat, not every line.

### 2E. On-the-nose emotion / thesis narrated aloud

The chapter repeatedly has characters say the theme instead of dramatizing it:
- Beat 2 Ronin (L335): "I'm done being the thing that takes." — states Act III's predator→protector thesis flatly. (The `voice:` note even calls it "the thesis made literal" — the subtext has become text.)
- Beat 3 Ronin (L397): "An hour ago I chose to guard a man instead of rob him. You're guarding too, Wraith." — narrates the structural parallel for the audience.
- Beat 4 Ronin (L487): "The thing in my skull was the prototype. Now everyone's the operative." — names the ladder beat outright.
- Beat 3 Sable (L427): "I've been wired into the cruelty of this place so long I'd forgotten there was another way to do a hard thing." — narrates her own interior shift.

Suggested direction: let one of these (e.g. L335) carry the thesis and make the others *implication* — Ronin can simply act and let Sable/Echo register it, rather than each character explaining the meaning of the scene they're in.

### 2F. Exposition / re-explaining the known

- Beat 0 Coral (L182 + L227): Coral delivers the same warning twice ("the deep is where they hide the kept" / "whatever you find in that water, it'll be one of us"). Redundant within one beat; collapse to one.
- Beat 0 (whole beat): the briefing restates Ch7/Ch8 context the crew already shares ("I told you in the reliquary…"). Some is needed for players; trim the in-crew recaps.
- Beat 3 Ronin (L432): explains his own make-lineage to Sable in a single mouthful — reads as a lore card. Could be shorter and more reluctant.
- Beat 4 Sable (L492) + Morrigan (L497): the reveal is necessarily expository, but it is delivered as two long uninterrupted lore-blocks with no friction or interruption between them.

### 2G. Uniform register (structural)

Gryph is written as a thirty-year warlord with a "hard gravel voice," yet he speaks in the same perfectly balanced rhetorical shapes as Echo, Coral, and Sable:
- L289: "you're either lost or you're here for the bottom shaft."
- L330: "used it for my people instead of against them."
- L340: "given freely than cut off your body" (mirrors Ronin's own L335 construction).

Recommendation: differentiate by *syntax*, not just stated accent. Gryph should be blunter, more fragmented, fewer matched clauses; Coral more hushed/liturgical; Echo wry and clipped. Right now they are interchangeable on the page with the speaker labels removed.

### 2H. Pet phrases / verbal tics across characters

- **Direct-address "Cipher"** is used as a line-anchor by Morrigan, Coral, Echo, Kessler, Sable (and implied to Gryph). It appears in the majority of lines and reads as a tic; thin it out by ~half.
- **"older make / same [cage|dark]"** motif is shared by Echo (L392 "same cage") and Ronin (L397 "Same dark."). Intended as a deliberate motif, but because two different speakers use the identical frame within seconds it reads as a writer's tic rather than a character's.

## 4. Prose summary — biggest recurring problems

The chapter is atmospheric and structurally sound on canon (the lineage, the shadow-AI lattice, the Wraith-keeper, the roster, and the ability chain all check out cleanly), so the consistency damage is light — one real continuity wrinkle (Gryph promises to dive, then is only a comm voice) and two cosmetic notes (Ronin calling the Cairn "my ship," and Gryph using "Cipher" before he's on-screen told it).

The naturalness problems are the real story, and they are systemic rather than line-local. **First, the antithesis reflex:** "not X, it's Y" is the default sentence shape of the whole script and shows up in almost every speaker, which both flattens the prose and makes everyone sound like the same essayist. **Second, register homogenization:** Gryph the gravel-voiced warlord, Coral the hushed keeper, Echo the wry witness, and Sable the doubled archive are written in the same balanced, epigrammatic cadence — with speaker labels removed, they are hard to tell apart. **Third, on-the-nose thesis:** characters keep *explaining the scene they are in* ("I'm done being the thing that takes," "you're guarding too," "the thing in my skull was the prototype"), so the emotional subtext is repeatedly converted to text. **Fourth, aphorism density:** nearly every line is engineered to be quotable, which paradoxically drains impact — when everything is a maxim, nothing lands as one. And two em-dashes slipped into character speech (L432, L492), which is a hard ban. The fix is largely subtractive: break the antithesis habit, let one line per beat carry the theme instead of all of them, and re-voice Gryph and Coral by syntax so the cast stops sounding like one narrator.
