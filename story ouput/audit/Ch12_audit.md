## 4. Summary of recurring problems

The chapter is structurally and canonically sound — the template reveal lands exactly as Ch12 requires, the codename rules hold, and (notably) there is not a single em-dash in character speech. The naturalness problem is **monotony of rhetorical device**, not slips. Three things recur:

1. **The "not X, Y" antithesis is the chapter's default sentence shape.** It appears in roughly eighteen lines across every single speaker — Cassie, Sable, Mera, Vess, Echo, Vale, Ronin. Because everyone uses it, the supposedly distinct voices (dry clerk, grand strategist, warm conscience, flat operative) collapse into one cadence. The fix is variety: let most characters break the pattern and reserve the device for one or two genuinely load-bearing lines.

2. **Aphorism-stacking — every beat wants to end on a quotable maxim.** Morrigan ends on three in a row; Ronin's refusal is a tricolon capped by a maxim; Vale, Echo, and Ronin all close on epigrams. Individually they're good lines; in bulk they read as written-to-be-screenshotted rather than spoken. Thin them so the maxims that remain actually hit.

3. **A couple of on-the-nose emotion beats** ("I don't fear him, Echo. I grieve him.") state the subtext the scene already carries. Trust the staging.

Where the writing *does* let characters stumble (Sable's catches, Echo's "And. Give me a second.") it's the chapter's most human and most convincing — that disfluency is the model the polished speakers should follow.
