## 4. Prose summary — biggest recurring problems

The chapter is genuinely well-built and, notably, **fully canon-compliant and entirely em-dash-free in speech** — the codename rollout (Echo says "Cipher" alone until the Mast, then the crew adopts it), the Hub-berthing rule, the Ally #2 trigger, and the shadow-AI mechanics are all handled exactly to spec.

The naturalness problem is one thing repeated everywhere: **the script writes in epigrams.** Almost every climactic line is engineered to be quotable, and the voice directions openly celebrate it ("the whole chapter's thesis in five words," "the chapter's thesis sealed"). Three tics drive it: (1) the **"not X, that's Y" antithesis** ("That's not a customs sweep. That's a hunt with a budget"; "That isn't a fault in the metal. That's a man"), which recurs across four different speakers and flattens their voices into a shared rhythm; (2) **aphorism-stacking**, where characters fire two maxims back-to-back (Kerrax: "Reasons are for buyers. I just collect." / "A killer that stops is a broken killer."); and (3) **theme-stated-aloud** at exactly the moments it should be left to subtext (Echo: "It was never a fault, Cipher. It's you. It always was"; Mera: "Nine years, hunting the wrong thing"). The fix is not to rewrite every line but to **let only one or two maxims survive per beat** and let the rest be ordinary, slightly messy speech — Tessa Rin and Mira already show the register the rest of the cast should occasionally drop into.

The one structural blemish is **redundant exposition**: Beat 1 (the Throat) re-delivers the neutral-ground/"they buy hands" and bounty-mechanic explanations the crew just heard in the Beat 0 briefing, to people who already know it. Compress the dock dialogue to reactions, not re-explanations.
