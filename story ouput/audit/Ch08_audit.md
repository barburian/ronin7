### Cross-character pattern findings (uniform register / shared tics)
- **Shared pet phrase "the living and the loud":** spoken by the Mourners (Beat 5, "you will not find it up here, among the living and the loud") and then repeated verbatim by Ronin-7 (Beat 5, "Not among the living and the loud"). One distinctive coinage handed between two characters reads as authorial, not diegetic. Fix: let Ronin paraphrase rather than echo the exact phrase ("Not up here, with the rest of us still breathing and shouting").
- **Mourners refrain "sight, not answers"** (Beat 4 "the Garden gives sight, not answers"; Beat 5 "We give sight, not answers; we told you that"). The in-text "we told you that" partly excuses the repeat, but two near-identical maxims in one chapter is one too many.
- **Ronin's "turn everything into a heading" tic** (Beat 0 "Plot it. Take us down."; Beat 4 "I've been hunting the wrong shape."; Beat 5 "So we stop hunting the surface. We go down."). This is his established character beat, but three closely-spaced "convert grief into a vector" lines flatten into a verbal formula; vary the phrasing on at least one.
- **Register uniformity (the core issue):** Mera (cold, economical hunter), Resh (clipped smuggler), Iris (early-20s tech), and the million-year-old Mourners all land quotable, perfectly balanced maxims. The flat-hunter and the smuggler in particular should sound rougher and less epigrammatic than the oracle. Recommend pulling Mera/Resh/Iris down toward plainer, more interruptible speech so the Mourners' formality reads as distinct rather than as the house style.

## 4. Summary — biggest recurring problems

1. **Saturation of balanced/aphoristic phrasing.** The chapter's signature weakness is that almost every line resolves into a quotable maxim, frequently via antithesis ("an order that lets you live with it, and an order that happened"; "Not that you broke. That you broke because...") or tricolon ("No walls. No guns. No garrison."; "The seals are right. The chain is right. The hand is right."). Individually these are well-crafted; in aggregate they are the strongest AI-tell in the script.

2. **Uniform register across very different characters.** A cold ex-Dominion tracker, a clipped smuggler, a 20-something tech, and a million-year-old soul-keeper all speak in the same elegant, perfectly-cadenced sentences. The Mourners' oracular voice should be the outlier, but the crew has been written up to its level, erasing the contrast. Roughen Mera, Resh, and Iris.

3. **A handful of shared coinages and self-echoes** ("the living and the loud" passed verbatim from Mourners to Ronin; "sight, not answers" repeated; Ronin's thrice-used "turn it into a heading / go down" move) that read as authorial habit rather than character.

**On the strong side:** zero em-dashes in dialogue (hard ban honored), and consistency is airtight — every codename, Garden-lore, and saga-fact check passes, with only one minor non-dialogue stage-direction wording nit. Naturalness, not canon, is where this chapter needs work.
