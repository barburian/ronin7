SETTING / ENVIRONMENT — WHERE THIS CHAPTER TAKES PLACE
Reference for art, lighting, level, and audio direction. Chapter 9 took him to the
bottom of a drowned world and showed him the machine the whole war is shaped like:
the Concord Engine, a leash being forged for the galaxy and strung through the kept
shadows of everyone the Program ever erased. He came up out of the Tide with a
sister of wire on his ship and one hard piece of knowledge from her mouth: the
Engine's build-record was never a single book. It was split across living archives
like her, scattered and buried, each carrying a section the Dominion did not trust
to one shelf. Sable is the map to them. She can feel the nearest one still strung
into the lattice, filed where the Dominion files everything it fears to have read,
which is down. Always down. Chapter 10 is the first node-run of Act III, the first
of three Sable can feel, and the only one she believes they bring up breathing.

The chapter unfolds in the NINEFOLD MINE SHAFTS: a nine-tiered vertical mine sunk
into a dead seam-world the Dominion gutted for the ore that builds its fleets and
then kept digging, long after the ore ran out, for a different kind of vault. Nine
descending tiers, and each one older and stranger than the last. The upper shafts
read as a working mine gone to rust: cut-stone galleries, dead lift-cages, ore-carts
seized on their rails, syndicate guard-posts squatting in the bones of an industry
that left. But the deeper the player drops, the wronger the architecture gets. The
honest mining gives way to something built to be unreachable on purpose: pressure
doors with no handles, signal-dead vaults wrapped in Program-original metal, tiers
where the very rock has been machined into a strongroom. This is not a mine anymore.
It is an accounts-house. The Dominion keeps the Engine's ledgers physically, deep
where no signal reaches, because a thing that cannot be transmitted cannot be stolen
by anyone who is not willing to walk all the way down to it. Paranoia made into
architecture, tier by tier, the deeper the truer.

At shaft seven the mine opens into THE ARCHIVE: a strongroom-cavern where the racks
of the reliquary stand again, but filed, indexed, catalogued, a library of the dead
strung into one living node. CASSIE-04 hangs in a rack of cabling at its heart, an
operative racked with the kept shadow-AIs of an entire generation — the Ninja make,
two programs older than Ronin's line — fused through her into one living node and made
its ledger. The shadows seated in her spine are Ninja, every one of them; the ledger she
reads runs wider, cross-indexed across the whole server to the true name and disposal
site of every operative the Program ever erased and to where the other scattered nodes
are kept. Where the Tide was a drowned hush, the Archive is a dry, cold, humming order,
the sound of a machine that has been counting the dead for a very long time and has
never once been interrupted. And far above all of it, back at the shaft-mouth where the
crew's salvage rig waits, a vengeance older than the mission has dug in at the one door
off this dead world, waiting for the man it has hunted across the galaxy to climb back
up into a blade's reach.

The chapter moves through six guided beats. After the Hub briefing it becomes a
descent and a reckoning: a drop down nine tiers of a strongroom-mine, a leashed-keeper
duel that frees a third inheritance, an archive who can finally name the dead, a
raider who chooses to spare instead of kill, and a target list that turns the
rebellion from a wound into a plan. The core loop is vertical traversal and combat
above, a guardian duel and a recruit at the Archive, a mercy-mirror at the way out,
and a reading of names that makes the endgame a map.

INTRUDING / RECURRING ELEMENTS
- THE DESCENT, AGAIN (the chapter's gravity): Chapter 9 established that every answer
  in this saga is further down than the last. The Ninefold honors that and twists it:
  this descent is not toward water but toward a vault, not the place the Program does
  its quiet work but the place it keeps its books. Treat the nine tiers as a count-down
  in stone, each one a held breath, the architecture growing more deliberate and more
  paranoid the deeper it goes. Down is still where the truth is buried. Here the truth
  is a list of the dead.
- THE STRONGROOM (paranoia made architecture): as the descent deepens, the honest mine
  yields to a thing built to be unreachable. Pressure doors with no handles, signal-dead
  metal, tiers machined into vaults. The Dominion keeps the Engine's accounts physically
  deep because a thing you cannot transmit is a thing no one can steal at range. Treat
  each sealed tier as proof that someone was terrified of these records being read, which
  is exactly why reading them matters.
- THE LEDGER (the ledger of the erased): Cassie's lattice is the ledger Ch5 has been
  hinting at since the first mention of accounts. Not a metaphor anymore: a true index
  of every erased operative, names and disposal sites, cross-referenced to the scattered
  nodes. When it opens, it should feel like a vault door swinging on the whole war. It
  becomes a hub system after this chapter, a searchable list of the taken.
- CASSIE-04 (the ledger-keeper): a living archive like Sable, but her own person. Where
  Sable is exhausted grief, Cassie is wry, dry, clear-eyed, a clerk who has been forced
  to file the dead for years and has kept her sanity by keeping her precision. She can
  carry the same faint archival doubling as Sable, but her affect is the opposite:
  gallows-dry, exact, a little funny in the way of someone who has survived by counting.
  She does not want pity. She wants the books finally read out loud.
- SEVER / NINJA-2 (the leashed keeper): the Ch10 equivalent of Vane, a survivor of the
  Ninja program — two makes older than Ronin's line — whose leash never broke, posted to
  guard the node. Like Vane he cannot be reasoned with or spared; the conditioning answers
  for him to the end. But keep him FRESH, not a copy: where Vane was an empty mirror that
  frightened Cipher with its absence, Sever has a flicker of something underneath the order,
  a man almost reaching the door before the leash hauls him back. The grief is the same
  brother-to-brother grief — across generations, an older make of the same cage; the texture
  is different. Tragedy in armor, never a sneer.
- VESS (the mercy-mirror): a hard, vengeance-driven raider whose people Ronin-7's old
  line destroyed. She is not soft and she does not become soft; her arc bends to mercy
  not because she forgives but because the names make killing him the smaller revenge.
  She spares him as a sentence, not a gift. Establish her as her own distinct voice:
  rough, fast, lethal, carrying a grief that has gone hard as flint.
- ECHO (the katana's shadow-AI): heard by Ronin-7 alone, wired to his optic feed. In
  Chapter 9 it inherited Vane's freed shadow and ran Overdrive. Here it inherits a
  third: Sever's freed blade-shadow floods into Echo and becomes Phase-step, Echo
  carrying and running the gift while the dead blade is left behind. The freed blade is
  not a second sword Cipher keeps; the ability is the inheritance. Echo is also the
  chapter's quiet dread about the names: it wonders, before anyone, whether its own kind
  and Cipher himself are filed in that ledger.

TONE OF PLACE
Chapter 9 was the descent into the worst room in the saga and the sizing of the war.
Chapter 10 is the turn from horror into operation. He came up out of the Tide knowing
the shape of the machine; now he goes down into the Ninefold to start taking it apart,
one node at a time, beginning with the node that can name the dead. The arc runs from
a Hub that finally has a plan, down nine tiers of a mine the Dominion turned into a
strongroom for its own crimes, to a second leashed brother he must kill and a second
sister he gets to save, to a raider waiting at the way out who has every right to take
his life and chooses instead to sentence him to spend it. He came down to read a list. He leaves
with the names of the taken, a step through walls, two allies, and a target list that
turns a rebellion into a campaign. The road out still runs deeper, toward a node in a
bone-canyon that reads wrong on the map, a sister Sable may not be able to save. The
predators have become protectors. Now, for the first time, they have a plan.
