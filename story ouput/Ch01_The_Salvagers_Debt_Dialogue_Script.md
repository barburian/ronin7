SETTING / ENVIRONMENT — WHERE THIS CHAPTER TAKES PLACE
Reference for art, lighting, level, and audio direction. The entire chapter
unfolds aboard a single location: Kessler's salvage rig, adrift in a dead
wreck-field. The rig is not a small ship — it is a derelict capital-class hull
Kessler salvaged and lives inside, but he has only ever powered a handful of
compartments; the vast majority of the leviathan stays cold, dark, and untoured
this chapter. Treat the LIVED-IN CORE as the fifth character — claustrophobic,
jury-rigged, a few warm rooms held together by a man who stopped caring about
comfort years ago, ringed by an enormous silence he doesn't go into.
(Full ship layout: 17_THE_HUB_Kesslers_Ship.md.)

THE WRECK-FIELD (exterior / backdrop, seen through viewports)
- A graveyard of dead hulls turning slow in the dark, lit by a single thin,
  dirty star. No planet, no traffic, no rescue. A hundred klicks of nothing in
  every direction. This is open black no one has reason to visit, which is why a
  sealed Dominion casket could be dumped here and stay lost.
- Light is sparse and cold: long shadows, a weak amber wash off the distant star,
  the occasional drift-glint of tumbling metal. The field should read as a place
  you bury things, not a place anyone lives.
- A single running light that ISN'T a wreck (Beat 2 close) is the first sign of
  the Dominion's arrival — steady, purposeful, wrong against the lifeless drift.

THE SALVAGE RIG — LIVED-IN CORE (interior — the whole playable stage of Chapter 1)
The few compartments Kessler keeps powered, welded into the guts of a far larger
dead hull: part workshop, part scrapyard, part home for one tired man. He works
the leviathan as a salvage cutter, carving up wrecks from a corpse-ship far too
big for one man to crew. Only this core is lit; everything past it is black,
sealed, and silent.
Recurring atmosphere across every room: coolant hiss, the slow tick of machinery
left running too long, the smell (implied) of ozone and old metal, gear that is
all secondhand, mismatched, and patched. Nothing here is clean. Nothing here is
new. Power is thin — flickers, shorted lines, sparks from stressed conduit.

  BEAT 1 — REVIVAL BAY (medical bay)
  - Welded into the guts of the rig; cramped, improvised, not a real med-bay.
  - A steel table at the center, secondhand monitors wired to it, an IV rack that
    clatters when knocked. Cheap medical gear, half of it live.
  - Tools that don't belong in a clinic: cutting torches, pry bars on a far
    workbench — this is a salvage cutter's space repurposed to keep a body alive.
  - On the workbench, conspicuously out of place: a plain katana.
  - Bulkheads close enough to pin a man against. Shorted lines throw sparks.
  - This is the room Ronin-7 wakes in; he returns one last look at it in Beat 4.

  BEAT 2 — MAIN HOLD
  - The largest interior space: salvage everywhere, sorted and half-stripped.
    Copper, stripped hull plate, other men's garbage in graded piles.
  - A grimy viewport looks out on the wreck-field — the chapter's main window.
  - Lived-in details: dented cups, a workbench edge worn smooth, habits of a man
    alone (he fills two cups out of reflex). Long-vigil fatigue soaked into it.

  BEAT 3 — AIRLOCK CORRIDOR + MEDICAL BAY (the boarding)
  - A cramped airlock corridor with inner and outer hatches. Red wash from a
    docking alarm; the heavy clank of a boarding clamp seating and a forced seal.
  - Tight, low-cover geometry built for a knife-range fight: the cutscene-to-
    combat flips here, fought through the medical bay and out into the corridor.
  - The medical bay (same room as Beat 1) becomes a kill-box: monitors, the now-
    empty table, no cover, one hatch in and one hatch out.

  BEAT 4 — CORRIDOR + COMMAND ROOM (the ultimatum)
  - A corridor feeding into the command room — space to walk and talk.
  - Command room: mismatched salvaged consoles, a captain's chair held together
    with strapping, a wide CRACKED viewscreen with the galaxy beyond it. Ronin-7
    catches his faint reflection in the broken glass.
  - In the corner, a battered ship-drone on a worn rail: one good optic, the
    personality of an old dog. The ship's only other "voice."
  - This is where the Handler's hologram blooms over the central console — the
    one moment outside intrudes directly into the rig's sealed little world.

INTRUDING ELEMENTS (the outside world pressing in)
- The Dominion: clipped vowels, visored troopers, streaming optics that send the
  fight up a wire in real time. They arrive by clamp and hatch; they do not knock.
- The Handler (V.O. / hologram): unseen until Beat 4, then a composed figure of
  light over the console. Cold courtesy. The only calm thing aboard, and the
  worst thing in the room.
- Velorum (referenced, never shown this chapter): the destination market where
  "they sell bodies and pasts and call it commerce." The course laid at the end.

TONE OF PLACE
Isolation, salvage, and a slow-running machine kept alive past its time — a fit
for a man kept alive past his. Everything is patched, dim, and secondhand until
the Dominion's clean, cold light cuts into it. The rig is safe only because it is
forgotten; the chapter is the moment it stops being forgotten.
